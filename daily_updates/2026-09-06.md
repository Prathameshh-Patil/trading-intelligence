## They agree on everything structural

Written independently, hours apart, against the same constraint. They land on the same ten
conclusions — including the same two module names (`instruments.py` with a `has_flow` boundary,
`features/portable.py`), the same portable surface (price, spread, quote rate, time), the same

**That convergence is worth more than either document.** Two passes landing on the same architecture
is the strongest evidence available here that it is forced by the problem rather than chosen, and

disagreement is only about which four strategies fill the slots.

## There is no quote data in this repo

The fact that decides two of the six slots, and neither document checked it.

```
data/2026-07/gc_trades.parquet
  cols: timestamp, price, size, aggressor_side, symbol, instrument_id
find data -name "*.dbn.zst"  ->  19 trades  ·  19 definition
```

**Every one of the 19 months is the `trades` schema. No `tbbo`, no `mbp-1`, no `bbo` — not one bid
or ask anywhere on disk.** The single TBBO pull that ever ran (`pull_tbbo_validate.py`, the

survives is `analysis/gc_footprint_quoterule_2026-07-15.csv`, aggregated to price levels and

And the two features are not one purchase. **Spread** needs `tbbo` (one row per trade) or `bbo-1s`
(a sample). **Quote rate is a count of book updates, so it needs `mbp-1`** — TBBO observes the book
only at trade times, so quote rate is not computable from it at all. `mbp-1` on GC is a different
order of magnitude from 46M trade rows and **nobody has run the cost estimate.** That is one command
and it should happen before any of this is scheduled.

Net: **four of the six candidates run today on data already paid for.** The two that do not —
Adverse Selection Filter and Path-Dependent Dynamic Exit — are both from `strategy-architecture.md`,
whose Phase 1 schedules them on Days 3 and 4.

## What the incoming spec gets right

Four things, three to adopt outright. Its **six-phase session taxonomy** (Asia, Asia-London, London,
London-NY, NY, NY-Asia) is better than the vague "minute buckets" in mine — the handoff phases are
the insight, and a fixed 15-minute binning would smear exactly the boundaries that matter. Its
**event table** is concrete where mine said "an event calendar" and stopped. Its **edge-case tables**
— DST, early close, holiday-thinned sessions, crossed markets, gap-through-stop — have no equivalent
in mine and are the sort of thing written only after something breaks.

And **"avoid negative EV rather than find alpha" is the better framing.** Against a null of −1.73 to
−6.42 ticks a leg before costs, the first-order win is not entering the bad ones. Mine argued for
non-directional strategies from the evidence and then still framed them as edge-seeking.

## Where it is wrong

- **`liquidity_score = (1/spread_z) * max(0, quote_rate_z)` is mathematically broken.** `spread_z`
  is a z-score, so it crosses zero; `1/spread_z` has a pole there and flips sign in the middle of the
  variable's normal range. Needs rewriting from the hypothesis, not patching.
- **Invented constants presented as pre-committed thresholds** — `SESSION_MULTIPLIER` at
  0.85/1.15/0.80, `vol_multiplier … * 0.3`, "+400% / +350% / +300%". Pre-committing a number you made
  up is not the same as pre-committing a threshold: pre-commitment guards against choosing a value
  after seeing the answer, it does not make a fabricated constant true. This repo's standard is
  stricter anyway — `strategies.py` and `regime_filter.py` carry **no defaults at all**, and
  `families.py` says it outright: *"No number in this file was authored here."* **The fix is a change
  of type:** the session effect is a measured table with an N per cell, not a multiplier.
- **§9 predicts `p_target` 0.22–0.25, a 10–25% lift.** `FAMILIES.md` measured that class of claim
  *yesterday* — −0.0058 and −0.0003, at power that would have caught a 30% relative lift. And the
  band straddles the 50+ bucket's 0.2237 breakeven, so its bottom half still loses money, which the
  document's own EV line concedes.
- **Kelly sizing and daily loss limits cross a frozen contract.** The pipeline design §2.3 is
  explicit: *"Position sizing is the user's, never ours … no field in §6's contract may become one."*
  That is the SEBI RA question `plans/current.md` carries as open pending Shreyas's CA call.
- **Smaller:** "Composite Score = S1 \* S2 \* S3 \* S4" multiplies a boolean by a bracket tuple, and
  the pseudocode below it multiplies nothing; "Full depth (L2)" appears in an *L1-Only* document;
  "correlation > 0.7" between two scalars is not a test; and its S2 calls (70,20,30m) a "base case"
  when `BASE_RATES.md` measured it at **−4.25 ticks** in that bucket — it is the null.
- **Its S4 (path-dependent exits) is a good idea blocked on resolution, not on a purchase.** "Move
  stop to BE+0.5 ATR once MFE > 1.5 ATR" depends on the **intrabar order** of the excursion and the
  retracement, and an OHLC bar does not record which came first — which is why `backtest.first_touch`
  resolves same-bar ties to the stop and every `p_target` in `BASE_RATES.md` is a stated lower bound.
  `regime_filter.py` already named this gap as *"exactly what D4 must measure at tick resolution."*
  That replay does not exist. It is buildable from data on disk, but it is real work, not "Day 4".
- **The 14-day roadmap ends in "production-ready"** with Day 12 being spot-feed integration — when
  C1 reopened yesterday, AllTick closed yesterday, and no spot vendor exists. The pipeline design
  handled this correctly by declaring itself **not scheduled**; the same move applies.

## Decided: the whole programme runs for nothing, and the fourth slot goes to spot

**Varad's call, 00:17.** The reconciliation originally proposed buying one session of GC `tbbo` to
settle the spread question. That purchase is off. Reconciliation §7 rewritten to record why, and §9's
order rewritten around it.

**The budget is zero, and only one thing was ever paid.** M1, M2, M3, anchor reversion and the
Path-Dependent Exit all run on the 46M trades already on disk. The only paid item in either document
was quote data for the Adverse Selection Filter — and only on GC.

**The correction worth reading twice is on the Path-Dependent Exit.** §5.6 called it blocked, and it
is — but on **tooling, not on a purchase.** Trade-by-trade replay needs `timestamp, price, size`,
which is precisely what every month on disk carries. It resolves the one thing an OHLC bar cannot
record — whether the favourable excursion or the retracement came first — and that unblocks both
`arch`'s S4 and the intrabar-stop fragility `regime_filter.py` flagged and nobody has measured.
**It is the largest single work item in the merged set and it costs nothing but time.**

**Route 2 chosen for the spread question, and the argument is not the price.** Three free routes were
on the table: Roll (1984)'s serial-covariance estimator on trades already held — free, but undefined
whenever that covariance is positive, which is common in trending intraday tape; trade intensity as a
stand-in for quote rate — free and honest, but not the same object as a book-update count; and route
2, get spot XAUUSD bid/ask where quotes are the entire feed.

**Route 2 wins because the problem inverts across the two instruments.** On GC, quotes cost money
*and* the variable is probably degenerate — GC sits at exactly one tick ($0.10) the overwhelming
majority of the time, which would make `spread_z` a z-score of a near-constant with unstable tails,
and `spread_z_max = 1.5` a threshold on noise. On spot, quotes *are* the feed: free, and spread is a
live moving variable. So route 2 tests the hypothesis on the instrument where it has a chance, using
the feed that gives it away. **Spending money to test something that may be structurally void on GC
was the wrong order.** The $0 is a side effect of that, not the reason.

**Two free spot sources, in order of preference — and the first is unverified.** Dukascopy publishes
XAUUSD ticks with bid and ask going back years, which is what makes a *historical* test possible
rather than only a live one. ⚠️ **Not verified from this machine.** `DELTA_CVD_FINDINGS.md` §4 once
recorded A2 as *"blocked on Dukascopy being unreachable"* — later rewritten because that was the
wrong reason for A2, but **the reachability itself was never retested.** Step one of route 2 is
confirming a download completes, before anything is built on it. Second source: an MT5 demo account,
free, live L1 plus exportable tick history — which doubles as the platform check open since Day 2.

**The caveat that survives either source, and it is not small.** Spot XAUUSD spread is a **broker
pricing decision, not a market outcome** — a dealer widens on its own risk policy and its own client
flow, so two brokers disagree about the same instant. The spot result is a legitimate yes/no on
*whether spread structure predicts anything*. It is **not** a threshold that transfers to GC or to
another broker, and it must not be read as one.

**M3's calendar is public too** — BLS publishes the NFP and CPI release schedules, the Fed publishes
FOMC dates. No vendor, no subscription.

**New order, all free:** M1 → M3 → M2 → tick replay + Path-Dependent Exit → route 2 spot spread → M4
decided. Steps 1–3 are days and need no new data at all; step 4 is the one that could run long, and
putting it fourth means three kill conditions have had their chance to fire before anyone commits to
building a replay engine.

**The `mbp-1` question is deferred, not answered.** If route 2 says spread structure is real, that is
the moment to price GC quote data — with an answer in hand about whether it is worth anything. Both
`pull_tbbo_validate.py --estimate-only` and `pull_futures_trades.py`'s cost-estimate flow return a
number **without spending anything**, so the estimate stays available for free at any time.

0.84× in 2026-03** — a five-fold range. `thresholds_selector.md` Part B's 70/20 was not one trade
across the archive, it was several wearing one name. That is ARCHITECTURE §4.6's pooling error, in
this repo's own data, and it decided the shape of all three numbers.

### 1 · M1's geometry grid

```
target  ∈ {0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0} × the bar's atr_bp
stop    ∈ {0.5,  0.75, 1.0, 1.5}          × the bar's atr_bp
horizon ∈ {15, 30, 60} minutes                        = 72 points per cell
```

**Multiples of the bar's own ATR, not bp and not ticks** — that is the only form of the surface that
means the same thing in every bucket and can transfer to spot. The span brackets every month's
equivalent of 70/20. No 5-minute horizon: `horizon.py` measured the median 5m |move| at 17 ticks
against a median ATR of 41.5, so even the smallest target is a tail event there.

**The pass line, all three required:** n ≥ 400 · EV − cost > 0 at the 1.4-tick round trip ·
`p_target` > `mde_rate(cell_null, n)`. **The deliverable is the shape of the surface, not its
maximum** — one grid point clearing the line inside a flat surface is a multiple-comparisons
artefact and gets reported as one.

**The prediction, written down before the run:** no cell clears the cost floor. `horizon.py` says
random walk from two independent directions, `BASE_RATES.md` says every bucket negative with the gap
to breakeven narrowing monotonically and never closing, `FAMILIES.md` says flat at power. If
something does clear it, expect it at targets ≤ 1.0× where `p_target` is high and cost eats most of
it. Being wrong there is the useful outcome.

### 2 · `atr_bp` bucket edges — `(0, 7, 10, 14, ∞)`

The archive's pooled quartiles rounded to whole basis points. Pooled shares **0.270 / 0.259 / 0.227
/ 0.245**. Rounding costs two points of balance and buys a number nobody has to defend — 6.79 looks
fitted even when it is not. It is in `features/portable.py` as `ATR_BP_EDGES`, and
`tests/test_instruments.py` fails if it drifts, so editing it means going back and amending the
commitment.

**⚠️ The mix moves violently and the sweep must be pooled, never averaged per month.** 2025-08
spends **69%** of its bars in `<7`; 2026-03 spends **66%** in `14+`. That is BASE_RATES §3's lesson,
louder.

### 3 · `reach.py`'s `MIN_SAMPLES` — **400**

Derived, not picked. A cell may quote a probability only if its N can separate "tradeable" from "the
null": the pooled archive null is **0.1644** and the highest per-bucket breakeven in it is **0.224**,
so `horizon.n_for_rate(0.1644, 0.224)` = **327**, rounded up to 400. There `mde_rate` = 0.2181 and
the quote carries ±3.9 pp.

**At the existing floor of 30 a cell needs a +126% relative lift before it can say anything** — and
nothing in this repo has ever produced one near that; `FAMILIES.md`'s best arm was +0.0184. A 30-leg
cell is noise with a number attached. `backtest.MIN_SAMPLES = 30` stays put as Track A's *reporting*
flag; the serving floor is a different object.

On GC the key is 144 cells over ~220k legs — ~1,500 each if they were even, and §2 says they are
nowhere near. **Many cells will return `forecast: null`, and that is the correct product behaviour**,
not a gap. The failure this stops is a confident 61% off forty legs, which looks exactly like a good
forecast on screen and is the cheapest way to destroy the calibration curve.

### One engineering constraint, named rather than discovered later

The sweep as committed is ~**32 million** `first_touch` iterations, and `first_touch` is a Python
loop over `bars.loc` slices. **Measure it on one month before launching 19.** If the projection runs
past a few hours, vectorise it first — a shared-spine change, additive, Track A's tests as the gate,
and it must not touch the tie-to-stop semantics that make every `p_target` a lower bound.
