## The finding

**`planfortoday.md`'s success metric was the break-even line.**

It sets `hit_70_rate ≥ 25%`, reasoning from 3.5:1 R/R break-even at 23%. For a driftless random walk
the probability of touching +70 before −20 is exactly `b/(a+b)` = **22.22%** — and break-even at
3.5:1 is `1/(1+3.5)` = **22.22%**. They are the same number, and necessarily so: **a fixed
target/stop pair on a driftless random walk always lands exactly on its own break-even.**
`horizon.py` measured this month to *be* a random walk at 5, 15 and 30 minutes, so 22.2% is what the
tape hands over for free. Add ~$25 of round-turn cost on a $700/$200 pair and break-even is 25.0%
exactly — the target as written.

Worse, at the 50–150 signals per month the file plans for, the smallest hit rate separable from that
null at 2σ is **34.0% / 30.5% / 29.0%** at N = 50 / 100 / 150. A true 25% edge could not be
demonstrated on one month however real it was.

So the pre-written threshold committed today is a **margin over a measured null**, not an absolute
rate, with `N ≥ 100` and *inconclusive* kept as a first-class outcome. `backtest.random_entries`
already exists and is what measures the null — nothing new is written for it.

**This is the third independent argument for buying more months.** `horizon.py` reached it from
variance, §6 of the design from cell counts, this from binomial power. Six months at roughly $15
brings the provable bar down to 25–27%. It is item 4 of today's gate and it is gated on the vendor,
not the money.

## The literature, triaged

Late addition, no code: [`docs/research/2026-08-28-gold-literature-triage.md`](../docs/research/2026-08-28-gold-literature-triage.md).
`Gold Quant Trading Research Papers.pdf` was read end to end and triaged against what
`services/signal-data` actually is, rather than against gold trading in general.

**It is an AI-generated survey, not research.** One entry's ORB claim rests on a studocu bachelor
thesis, three citations are Scribd re-uploads, one is a broken viewer URL to a price-forecast page,
and Pillar 5's headline returns ("171% in three months") trace to preprints with no described
out-of-sample discipline. **The 53 URLs on pp.21–23 are the only load-bearing part.** Same posture
as the version-lock PDF.

Three things in it are worth acting on: **realized semivariance** (`RS⁺`/`RS⁻`) as a fifth Stage 1
candidate, computable from the parquet we already own and shaped like the four already in
`strategies.py` · **`PMC9759686`**, free and peer-reviewed and the only cited paper working at 1–15
minutes · and the **macro-announcement check** — if Stage 1 entries cluster on CPI/NFP bars, a
5-minute edge is an event artifact rather than an order-flow one, which matters precisely because
Day 5 measured this month as a random walk at 5m. Tagging entries by distance to the nearest release
is cheap and belongs before Part B is judged.

**p.20 says the same thing this morning's finding said, from the other direction.** `backtest.py`
reports ticks gross, and the correct fix for a threshold-triggered rule is to widen the threshold to
cover the round trip rather than subtract cost from the result at the end — the second leaves the
entry population unchanged and reports trades that were never worth taking. The ~$25 round turn that
moved break-even to 25.0% above is the same number, and it belongs in the entry rule.

**The larger finding is what the PDF leaves out.** Five pillars and none of them is market
microstructure, which is what this project is. `absorption` is Kyle's lambda inverted, `delta_z` is a
coarse order-flow imbalance, `absorption_fade` is flow toxicity / VPIN, `backtest.evaluate` is the
triple-barrier method with only the vertical barrier, and `pull_tbbo_validate.py` is Lee–Ready — so
the 99.65% agreement has a published comparison set it can be judged against. §4 of the note carries
the primary sources.

**And one of them corrects a number committed earlier today.** Thirty-minute horizons on five-minute
bars produce heavily overlapping labels; those observations are not independent, so the **effective
N is below the nominal N** and the separable hit rates above (34.0% / 30.5% / 29.0%) are
**optimistic rather than conservative**. That is a fourth independent argument for buying more
months. The correction is mechanical — sample-uniqueness weighting, and purged-and-embargoed splits
for the §6.4 half-split, which leaks across its boundary for the same reason. **Neither is
implemented and neither has been benchmarked.**

**Caveat carried into the note itself:** §4 is cited from knowledge. Nothing in it was fetched or
verified against a publisher in this session, so the titles and years are search terms, and each DOI
needs checking before any of it is quoted to Shreyas or a vendor.

## The pull that would also have bought NQ

Acting on the triage's recommendation to buy months before the gap surfaced a live defect in
`pull_futures_trades.py`: **it has no instrument filter.** `estimate_cost` and the run loop both
iterate `INSTRUMENTS.items()`, which is GC *and* NQ, and the docstring said so as though it were
intended. Six months would have cost **~$83 instead of ~$21** and bought an instrument dropped on
25 Aug. **The $30 safety threshold does not catch this** — it is per-run, and a GC+NQ month lands
near $14, so the guard passes and the money goes quietly.

Fixed with one flag: `--instrument`, `nargs="+"`, `choices={GC,NQ}`, **default `["GC"]`**, and
`estimate_cost` now takes the roots it was asked for instead of every root it knows. +29/−7 lines.
**72 tests pass, `ruff` and `mypy` clean, and it is verified against the live API** — six
`--estimate-only` calls each printed one `GC` line and no `NQ` line. That is the behaviour the flag
exists for, and it is the part that is proven.

Six months (Jan–Jun 2026, making **Jan–Jul contiguous** with the month already held) estimate at
**$21.12** — $5.51 / $3.10 / $4.32 / $2.73 / $2.65 / $2.82 — against $125 of unspent free credit.
That is above the ~$15 this morning's gate note assumed; the heavier months are GC's active-month
cycle rather than an error, and the number in the gate note should be read as $21, not $15.
