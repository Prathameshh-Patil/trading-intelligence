### The bug the tests caught, which is the reason to write tests first

The EMA gate originally compared `sign(close − ema)` against a **one-bar** slope. A test asserting a
falling market fails alignment blew up, and chasing it produced the actual result:

> `ema_t` always lies between `close_t` and `ema_t−1`. So `close > ema_t` and `ema_t > ema_t−1` are
> the *same statement*, and the two signs can never disagree.

The gate was a **tautology** — it would have passed every bar except an exactly flat one and filtered
nothing at all, while looking entirely reasonable in the pass-rate table. The slope is now measured
over `span` bars, where the two quantities are genuinely independent. The case it now rejects — price
rallying back above an average that is still below where it was — is a bounce inside a downtrend, and
there is a test pinning it.

Worth noting the failure mode: this would not have shown up as a crash, a type error or an
implausible number. It would have shown up as a filter that quietly did nothing.

## The regime plot, reviewed — and D1's gate was wrong

D1's `survival >= 0.92` was implemented as specified and flagged as "Varad's call, not a bug to patch."
That was the wrong place to stop: `regimes_2026-09-02`'s own README says the run is unreviewed, and the
gate was accepted anyway. Reviewed now, before D3 consumes it.

### What the plot shows

Regimes 0 (blue) and 1 (orange) never form contiguous blocks. They appear as speckle *inside* green
stretches, clustering on the steep CVD legs — 0 on rising CVD, 1 on falling. Green (regime 2) is
everywhere else and is most of the chart.

That picture is consistent with both readings — genuinely unstable states, or stable states that are
simply rarer — so the transition structure had to settle it.

### It settles decisively: they are stable, and the threshold was measuring prevalence

**Raw survival is confounded by base rate.** A regime occupying 63% of bars scores 0.63 by shuffling
alone; one occupying 15% scores 0.15. An absolute threshold on survival is therefore a threshold on
*how common a regime is*, wearing a different name.

Normalising — `kappa = (P(stay) − share) / (1 − share)`, Cohen's kappa against a shuffled-label null —
**inverts the ranking**:

| regime | share | P(stay) | vs chance | **kappa** | E[run] |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 15.4% | 0.866 | **5.61×** | **0.842** | 7.5 bars |
| 1 | 21.5% | 0.875 | **4.07×** | **0.833** | 8.0 bars |
| 2 | 63.0% | 0.927 | 1.47× | 0.798 | 13.7 bars |

**The transition matrix rules out instability.** `0→1 = 0.0043`, `1→0 = 0.0023`. The two flow regimes
essentially never alternate with each other; they decay into 2 (0.129, 0.122) and come back. The
churn-between-opposite-flows failure mode simply is not there.

**And they are not high-variance.** `price_efficiency` sd: **0.243 / 0.233 / 0.230** — identical.
`cvd_persistence` sd: 0.200 / 0.191 / 0.153. On the clustering's own features, 0 and 1 are no noisier
than 2.

Meanwhile **regime 2 is the residual bucket**: `cvd_persistence` 0.206 against 0.61,
`price_efficiency` 0.341 against 0.54, `cvd_slope` −0.199 with sd 11.4 — noise around zero. So the gate
kept the one state where nothing coherent is happening, and discarded both that carry flow, for a
strategy family built on flow.

### What changed

`regime_filter.py` gates on **kappa**, not raw survival. `regime_survival()` stays as the raw input and
a reportable diagnostic. **The 0.92 number is retired**, with the reasoning written into
`analysis/regimes_2026-09-02/README.md` where the labels live. Two new tests: one asserting kappa
reverses a raw-survival ranking that favours the common regime, one asserting a single-regime frame
gives NaN rather than dividing by zero. 99 tests green.

All three regimes clear `kappa >= 0.75` on the training half — 0.842, 0.833, 0.798. **So on this
labelling the regime dimension carries no filtering information at all.** That is the honest result: the
clustering separates *what the market is doing*, but not *whether the label will hold*.

| `atr_min` | bars | pct |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 40 | 2,409 | **38.4%** |
| 45 | 1,879 | 29.9% |
| 48 | 1,573 | 25.1% |

## `atr_min` derived from first principles — and the proposed reason was backwards

`atr_min = 40` was the last threshold in the filter chosen by looking at the pass rate. The proposal
was to derive it as **stop × safety factor**: 20-tick stop, 1.5–2×, therefore 30–40, and 40 is 2× the
stop.

**The number is right. The mechanism is inverted**, and documenting it that way would have baked in a
reason that fails on reading:

> "ATR ≥ 2× the stop, ensuring sufficient volatility for the stop to function as intended."

A larger ATR does not help the stop. At ATR 40 a 20-tick stop is roughly **half a typical bar**
(median 5-minute range 38 ticks), so raising the volatility floor makes the stop **more** likely to be
taken out by intrabar noise, not less. It is the same fragility D4 has to resolve at tick resolution,
and a volatility floor is not the instrument that fixes it.

### What the floor actually protects: the target

The 70-tick target has to be reached inside D4's **6-bar / 30-minute** forward window. That gives a
real constraint, and it is measurable without touching outcomes.

Over every window in the **training half** — no entries, no direction, no hit rates, just how far the
tape travels in 6 bars, the same class of statistic as the median bar range:

| ATR bucket | n | median best one-way move | P(move ≥ 70) |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 25–30 | 224 | 58.5 | 35.7% |
| 30–35 | 384 | 63.0 | 43.2% |
| 35–40 | 476 | 66.0 | **45.8%** |
| **40–45** | 472 | **75.5** | **55.3%** |
| 45–50 | 393 | 82.0 | 65.4% |
| 50–60 | 447 | 93.0 | 72.3% |

**Solving for where the median best one-way move equals the 70-tick target: ATR = 39.4** on the
training half, **40.1** on the full month. The crossover from "worse than a coin flip" to "better" sits
between the 35–40 and 40–45 buckets.

So the floor is where a signal stops being asked for a move the tape does not usually supply in the
time allowed. **40 ticks, derived, and it agrees with the number already in the code to a decimal.**

That it also equals 2× the 20-tick stop is a numerical coincidence worth noting and not worth relying
on — the two quantities are the same size, for unrelated reasons.

### The wording, corrected

> **`atr_min = 40` ticks.** The volatility floor at which the 70-tick target becomes reachable inside
> D4's 6-bar (30-minute) forward window: measured over the training half, the median best one-way move
> across 6 bars equals 70 ticks at ATR ≈ 39.4. Below it the target is worse than a coin flip against
> the horizon. The 20-tick stop is not what this protects — at ATR 40 the stop is about half a typical
> bar, so more volatility makes it *more* noise-prone, which is D4's problem to measure and not this
> gate's to fix.

Written into `features/regime_filter.py`'s module docstring, where the gate is.

## W1D3, four days early — `signals/engine.py`, and a gate that cannot fire

Varad's D3 is Mon 7 Sep. Written tonight because Parts B and D both landed and nothing else gated it.

**The number D3 asks for, and it is a zero: the 3-of-4 checker produces 0 signals on 3,516 training
bars.** Not one. The design is arithmetically dead before any threshold is argued about, and the
reason is worth more than the signal count would have been.

### What was built

`services/signal-data/signals/engine.py`, 156 lines. Four conditions in `strategies.py`'s shape —
`(bars, *, thresholds) -> +1 / -1 / 0` — plus a combiner:

| condition | rule | fires, unfiltered, on 3,516 training bars |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| **A** absorption | **stub, returns zeros** — `absorption_fade` was dropped in Part B | 0 · 0.0% |
| **B** delta divergence | new closing low for the window, on less selling than the window's worst bar, delta already turning up | 405 · 11.5% |
| **C** CVD momentum shift | trailing CVD slope crosses from non-positive to positive (or the mirror) | 386 · 11.0% |
| **D** range expansion | bar ≥ 1.5 × ATR wide with ≥ 60% of that range in its body | 213 · 6.1% |

Every feature is imported from `features/`; nothing is re-derived. **No threshold has a default** —
the same `TypeError` enforcement `strategies.py` and `regime_filter.py` use — and `eligible` is a
required argument, so the regime filter cannot be left off by accident. **122 tests pass** (103
before — 19 new), `ruff` and `mypy` clean across 27 source files.

Windows are borrowed, not invented: B and C run on `"60min"` / `min_bars=6`, which is
`cvd_divergence`'s committed Part B window; D's ATR is `"70min"` / 14, which is Part D's. The two
ratios in D — 1.5 × ATR, 60% body — are `planfortoday.md`'s own numbers for condition D. **No number
in this run was authored by Claude.**

### Why the gate is empty, and it is not sparsity

The obvious explanation is that three conditions firing at ~11%, ~11% and ~6% rarely coincide. That
is true and it is not the whole answer:

| pair | co-fire on the same bar | **agree on direction** |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| B + C | 34 | 17 |
| C + D | 33 | 29 |
| **B + D** | **26** | **0** |
| B + C + D | **0** | 0 |

**B and D can never agree, by construction.** B fires long at a *new closing low*; D fires long only
on a bar that *closed up on a big body*, which at a new low it is not. Twenty-six bars have both
conditions live and **zero** have them pointing the same way — and that is not a small-sample
accident, it is what the two rules mean.

So "3 of 4" was never four interchangeable votes. With A silent, 3-of-4 **requires B and D to agree**,
which cannot happen. Even ignoring that, B+C+D co-fire on **0** bars against **2.7 expected** if the
three fired independently, and ~0.7 after requiring direction agreement. The gate would have been
empty or near-empty even with a compatible triple.

**This is the consequence Part B flagged and it is being reported, not relabelled.** Dropping
`absorption_fade` made the combiner 3-of-3; what the drop actually did was leave a triple containing
a contradictory pair.

### The 2-of-4 reading, and what the filter does to it

Relaxing to 2-of-4 — reported as a diagnostic, **not adopted**; `min_conditions` stays a required
argument with no default:

- **46 signals unfiltered**, 24 long / 22 short, split **17 × B+C** and **29 × C+D**. The B+D cell is
  empty, as above.
- **1 signal filtered.** One. The regime filter removes 45 of 46.

Which gate removes them is the finding:

| gate | passes, of the 46 signal bars | passes, of all bars |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| `cvd_persistence ≥ 0.40` | **3 of 46** | 41.1% |
| `atr ≥ 40` | 28 of 46 | 59.2% |
| `EMA(15)` aligned | 30 of 46 | 66.4% |
| `kappa ≥ 0.75` | 46 of 46 | 95.9% |
| session interior | 45 of 46 | 99.3% |
| **all five** | **1 of 46** | 18.3% |

**The persistence gate and the signal conditions want opposite things.** B and C both select bars
where flow is *turning* — a divergence is delta failing to confirm, a shift is the slope changing
sign — and turning flow is cancelling flow, which is exactly what low CVD persistence measures. A
gate added on 2026-09-04 to put the filter's discrimination back onto a flow quantity turns out to be
anti-correlated with two of the three surviving conditions: it passes 41% of all bars and 6.5% of
signal bars.

**Nothing is being tuned in response.** Part D is frozen and its criterion 2 — filtered vs unfiltered
`hit_70_rate` — is the test that decides whether this interaction is a problem or the filter doing
its job. That is D4's measurement, not tonight's.

### Clustering: better than the plan feared

D3 warns that 90 signals in four sessions is not 90 observations. The 46 unfiltered 2-of-4 signals
spread across **all 13 training sessions**, max 8 in one, top three sessions holding 20 of 46 (43%).
Clustering is not what is wrong here — **N is**, and it is wrong at the design level rather than the
sample level.

### The free test case: 2026-07-16, 08:00 ET

The Week 1 gate line: price fell 47.7 points that hour on delta **+1,083**, and *if nothing flags it,
Stage 1 does not work*. With absorption gone, Part B put that on `delta_outlier`.

**`delta_outlier` flags it — three of the twelve bars in the hour** (12:05, 12:15 and 12:45 UTC), at
Part B's committed `window="120min"`, `min_bars=12`, `z=2.0`. **The gate line is met.**

Two things sit behind that pass and neither is a formality:

1. **All three flags are long, into a 47.7-point drop.** `delta_outlier` is a continuation rule, so
   it reads +1,083 contracts of buying as buying. Flagging the hour and taking the right side of it
   are different achievements, and this hour is precisely the case that decides **Decision 1 — fade
   or continuation** — which Part B recorded and deliberately did not take.
2. **The engine does not flag the hour at all.** In those twelve bars B fires long three times, D
   fires short twice, C never fires, and 9 of 12 bars pass the filter. The B/D opposition, on the
   exact hour that motivated the test.

### What was deliberately not run

**The held-out half was not touched.** Everything above is the training half — ≤ 2026-07-19, 13
sessions, 3,516 bars. Part D's second honesty claim is that the held-out half has not been looked at
*through the filter*; counting filtered signals on it tonight would have spent that claim for a
number D4 and D5 need more.

One number that does bear on an existing prediction, reported as a raw count and nothing more:
**`delta_outlier` produces 221 entries on the training half alone**, against Part B's recorded
prediction that it lands **under N = 100** unfiltered. Entry counts and a backtest's N are not the
same quantity — overlap and horizon handling sit between them — but this is the first evidence on
that prediction and it points the other way.

### The decision this hands back, and it is not Claude's

D4 needs a signal set. Right now it would receive **0 signals at 3-of-4** and **1 at 2-of-4
filtered**, and neither is a thing to measure MFE/MAE on. Three options, in the order they cost:

1. **Run D4 on the 46 unfiltered 2-of-4 signals** and report filtered against unfiltered as Part D's
   criterion 2 asks. Cheapest, and the filtered arm is a single signal — a comparison in name only.
2. **Restore condition A** as a bar-level absorption rule. It needs one number nobody has committed
   (how close to VWAP counts as "at a level"), and that number is Varad's, not Claude's.
3. **Treat the opposition as a specification error.** The four conditions are two families, not four
   votes: A+B read exhaustion, C+D read continuation. A combiner over a family that can agree is a
   different engine, and it is a design change rather than a threshold change.

**Nothing here is a threshold to tune, and none of the three is a call this session should make.**

## AllTick — what the API actually serves, and what the key reaches

Varad supplied an AllTick token and asked what data it gives. Nothing about this touches
`services/signal-data`; it is a **vendor survey**, and it lands next to the C1 feed decision
rather than inside the Week 1 lane.

New directory `alltick/` — catalog extractor, four probe scripts, a site builder and
`site/index.html`. 980 lines, no dependency outside the standard library except `websockets`
for the stream probe.

*Amended 5 Sep, since this file is otherwise append-only: the HTML site was dropped and its
published link removed at Varad's request — see [`2026-09-05.md`](2026-09-05.md).
[`alltick/GOLD.md`](../alltick/GOLD.md) is the surviving record.*

### The catalog is not in the docs

AllTick's GitHub docs ship a **truncated sample** of each product list — a dozen rows per class —
and point at a Google Sheet for the rest. `packages/catalog/extract.py` pulls that workbook and
parses all nine tabs: **11,223 instruments**, of which 5,086 are A-shares, 2,962 US and 2,870 HK.

Three parsing bugs were caught by looking at the output rather than by reasoning about it, and
each would have produced a plausible-looking catalog:

1. The **crypto tab has no Name column** — its header is `Code | Transaction hour` — so a
   fixed column offset put the trading-hours paragraph in every crypto instrument's name.
2. The **KRW block in the FX tab has no Category column**, so a header-derived column map lost
   all ten of its codes.
3. The **banner row repeats at the bottom of five sheets** and was being counted as an
   instrument. The reported total was 11,228 until that was fixed.

Codes are now found by shape (short, space-free, ASCII) rather than by position.

### What the token actually reaches: 10 of 11,223

**This is the finding that matters for any vendor decision.** Entitlement is per product, not per
plan tier, and unauthorised codes are **dropped silently** from a mixed request — only a request
where *every* code is unauthorised returns `604 code unauthorized`. Scanning five at a time (the
free-plan per-request cap) isolates exactly which codes answer.

**311 codes probed, 10 answered:** `USDJPY`, `GOLD`, `USOIL`, `HK50`, `BTCUSDT`, `TSLA.US`,
`700.HK`, `000001.SH`, `399001.SZ`, `.DJI.US`. The free plan is **one sample instrument per asset
class**, not a sample of every instrument. Nothing about the 11,223 is reachable on this key.

The scan swept forex, metals, energy, CFD indices and market indices whole, and sampled 50 codes
from each of the three equity books. **A closed market looks identical to a denied one**, so a
miss is recorded as "no data for this token right now", not as a proven permission error.

### Undocumented behaviour worth carrying

- **The rate limit does not use the documented envelope.** Free plan is one request per ten
  seconds per endpoint; exceeding it returns HTTP 429 with `{"error_msg": "Too many requests"}` —
  no `ret`, no `msg`. A client that reads `ret` without checking the status code sees a malformed
  success rather than a rate limit. Two probe runs were wasted on a 7-second gap before this was
  identified; the `ret=None` rows in those runs were rate-limit rejections, not API responses.
- **`/static_info` is stock-host only.** On `quote-b-api` it answers **HTTP 200** with the
  plain-text body `Unable to find matching target resource method`. There is no reference data
  for forex, metals or crypto.
- **2h and 4h candles on equities return `ret=200` with an empty `kline_list`**, not an error —
  matching the docs' claim, but failing open.
- **`/kline` caps at 500 candles and GOLD only has 357 daily bars** (2025-04-21 to 2026-09-04).
  Deeper history means walking `kline_timestamp_end` backwards, which only forex, metals and
  crypto accept.
- **Book depth varies by venue, not by plan:** 10 levels on HK equities, 5 on crypto, 1 on spot
  metals, none on indices.

### What was verified, and how

`ruff` clean. The site was rendered headless in both themes and every section inspected as an
image — which is how the bar chart (inline `<span>` ignoring `height`), the mojibake (no charset
declaration) and the overflowing catalog table were each caught. **The WebSocket leg is the one
thing measured only once per host:** 235 pushes in 25 s on `GOLD`/`BTCUSDT`/`USOIL`, 402 on
`TSLA.US`; `700.HK` acked the subscription and pushed nothing, because HK was closed. A first
stock-socket attempt failed with a DNS error and succeeded on retry, so treat the socket as
"works", not as "measured for reliability".

**Not claimed:** no throughput or latency measurement, no test suite for the probe scripts, and
no attempt to price a paid tier. Whether AllTick is worth paying for is a decision for the C1
thread, and nothing here makes it.

## AllTick gold — L1 only, 1 Hz, and a dead aggressor flag

Varad cut the question down to the only part that matters: **does AllTick give gold
L1, L2, MBP or MBO?** Full memo in [`alltick/GOLD.md`](../alltick/GOLD.md), raw capture in
`alltick/data/gold.json`, reproducible with `make gold`.

Measured over **two independent windows three hours apart** (240 s at 19:42 IST, 180 s at
23:08), because a single capture on a one-second feed is a thin basis for a vendor call.

**The answer is L1 only, and even that is degraded.** Three measurements, each one
independently disqualifying, and both windows agree on all three:

1. **The book is 1×1 on every update.** Never any other shape, across the whole capture
   window, on both the WebSocket push and REST `depth-tick`. No second level ever appears.
2. **The stream ticks about once per second** — book and trades both — and every
   `tick_time` is second-aligned. The venue-side `seq` counter advances **52.4/second** in
   window A and **33.6/second** in the quieter window B, against **0.99 and 0.91 delivered**:
   **1.9% and 2.7% of ticks arrive.** The delivered rate did not move when the market
   quietened, which is what a fixed cap looks like rather than a feed with less to send.
   What is labelled a tick is a one-second bucket.
3. **`trade_direction` is hardcoded to 1** across all 402 trade pushes in both windows —
   including **all 202 down-ticks**. Price fell about as often as it rose and the flag never
   once said SELL. It carries no information.

**None of the three is a free-plan artefact, and that is the part worth keeping.** The same
key returns **10 book levels on `700.HK` and 5 on `BTCUSDT`**, so depth is venue-shaped, not
tier-shaped; and `TSLA.US` pushed **16 updates/second**, so the 1 Hz ceiling belongs to the
gold feed specifically. What was *not* tested is whether a paid tier is a different gold feed
— nobody priced one.

### Why this closes the question rather than deferring it

`trade-tick` and `depth-tick` are **latest-value only** — no timestamp parameter, no history
for either. So there is no bid/ask history to reconstruct aggressor side from the way
`pull_tbbo_validate.py` did with the quote rule, and no book history to backtest against even
if capture started today. `/kline` is the only endpoint that goes backwards, and it is OHLCV.

Two things line up with findings this repo already owns. **AllTick carries no CME futures at
all** — 11,223 instruments, no `GC`, no `MGC` — so `GOLD` is spot/CFD and was never a route to
the contract. And its quoted book sizes swing **4.6x between two snapshots 14 seconds apart**,
with bid and ask near-identical inside each one; that is an indicative number, not resting
size, which is the same no-centralised-volume problem **A2** closed spot gold on in August.

### One bug in the measurement code, caught before it mattered

The first version of the direction-vs-price check paired `Counter.elements()` against the price
series — an unordered sequence against an ordered one. It returned the right answer only because
every direction was identical; with a varying flag it would have been wrong. Rewritten to pair
`itertools.pairwise(prices)` against `pairwise(directions)` with `strict=True`, and re-run. The
conclusion is unchanged, but the earlier number was not evidence.

**Verdict:** context-only, and that box is already occupied. OHLCV across ten intervals and a
1 Hz mid-price are all it offers. Databento stays the historical archive and the live GC feed
stays with Ironbeam — **nothing here changes C1.**
