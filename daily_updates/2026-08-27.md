### The question, stated so it can be answered

Stage 1's kill gate asks whether any strategy beats a matched-count random-entry null (§6.3). That
is a hypothesis test, and a hypothesis test has power. **The horizon sets the noise the edge has to
clear**, so before asking whether an edge exists it is worth asking which horizons could show one.

This needs no threshold, which is why it was available today. Entering long on every bar is not a
strategy — it is the population the null draws from, and its dispersion is the floor. Every forward
move was measured **through `backtest.evaluate`**, so the leg semantics are the kill gate's own
rather than a second implementation that agrees by luck.

### The result

GC July 2026, 6,276 five-minute bars, 23 sessions:

| | 5m | 15m | 30m |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| σ of forward move | 34.53 | 59.86 | 84.68 ticks |
| σ ÷ √h | 1.000 | 1.001 | 1.001 |
| median \|move\| | 17 | 28 | 40 ticks |
| overlap ρ, adjacent bars | −0.009 | 0.659 | 0.831 |
| min detectable edge @ n=100 | 13.68 | 23.72 | 33.55 ticks |
| signals to prove a 3-tick edge | 2,081 | 6,251 | 12,507 |

**The price series is a random walk at these horizons, and two independent measurements say so.**
Dispersion grows as √h to within 0.1%. The overlap correlation between adjacent bars' legs lands at
0.659 and 0.831 against a random walk's predicted (h−5)/h = 0.667 and 0.833. Neither was fitted to
the other. Nothing unconditional is trending or reverting here — so **a longer horizon buys variance
at exactly the rate that makes an edge harder to prove, and returns no structure in exchange.**

Judged at 30 minutes, Stage 1 needs an edge **2.45× larger in ticks** than at 5 minutes to reach the
same significance. That is √6, and it is the whole story.

### The second penalty, which only bites the strategies we actually have

Adjacent 30-minute legs share 25 of their 30 minutes, so their outcomes are not independent draws.
For a rule firing a few times a day this is harmless — average spacing swamps the horizon. **It is
not harmless for order-flow rules, which fire in bursts by construction:** a delta-outlier or
absorption rule fires *during* the volatile stretch, and inside a burst the effective sample is far
below the count. `mde_n100` at 30m assumes 100 independent observations that a bursty rule does not
supply.

### Robustness, because one month and one instrument is not much

- **Both walk-forward halves scale identically** — 0.992–1.016 — while their volatility *levels*
  differ by 19% (σ₅ = 37.02 before the 19 Jul split, 31.09 after). The level moves; the exponent
  does not. That is the reassuring shape: had the exponent moved with the level, one month would
  have proven nothing.
- **The 2026-07-16 session rebuilt at 1-minute resolution** from the raw S1 fixture, through
  `s1.load_ticks` → `minute_bars` rather than the committed CSV: 1.000 / 1.011 / 1.019.
- **The 5-minute grid costs 1.1%** — that same session gives σ₅ = 32.31 on the 5-minute grid against
  31.96 on every minute. The stated limitation is now a measured one.

### The defect this turned up in `backtest.py`

`evaluate` NaN'd a leg only when it was *entirely* empty. A leg the session cut short — entered ten
minutes before the close, measured at a 30-minute horizon — was reported as a full 30-minute move.

It is small and it points one way: **2.2% of bars at 30m against 0.4% at 5m, every one of them
biased toward zero, at exactly the horizon under question.** A horizon whose session ends before it
does is now NaN, and the row is kept so a horizon that *did* complete is not thrown away with it.

**The fix made the result cleaner, which is the corroboration worth having.** σ ÷ √h moved from
1.000 / 0.998 / 0.993 to 1.000 / 1.001 / 1.001 — the truncation had been diluting exactly the
long-horizon dispersion it should have. Nothing was tuned to produce that.

Three tests were asserting the old behaviour and were rewritten rather than bent:
`test_a_horizon_never_runs_past_its_session` now proves non-leakage with a horizon that *completes*
inside its session, and the truncation case gets its own test next to it.

### The uncomfortable half of this result

**The kill gate is underpowered at every horizon, 5 minutes included.** With a plausible signal
count of 100–250, the smallest edge it can separate from the null is **8.65–13.68 ticks at 5m** —
$87 to $137 a trade. A real order-flow edge is one to three ticks. Proving a 3-tick edge at 5m needs
**2,081 signals** against a month holding 6,253 usable bars.

§6 predicted *inconclusive* and recorded it in advance. **That prediction is now confirmed
quantitatively, and it is worse than written:** §6 expected Stage 2's regime-strategy cells to be
too thin. Stage 1 is too thin on its own, before any regime split.

This is an argument for **more months**, and it is the same argument §6 already makes — one month
was $2.52 and six is ~$15. It is now backed by a specific number rather than an intuition: at 5
minutes, six months would put a 3-tick edge inside reach at ~2,000 signals. **At 30 minutes six
months would not be enough**, and neither would twelve.

### What I did not let this claim

- **This says nothing about whether an edge exists.** It measures the instrument, not the market. A
  strategy could clear 13 ticks; nothing here says it cannot.
- **The MDE is a pessimistic bound.** Strategy and null are drawn from the same month and their
  entries overlap, so a properly paired test has somewhat more power than the two-sample figure.
- **The cost floor is an assumption, not a measurement** — 1 tick of spread/slippage plus ~$4
  commission, 1.4 ticks round trip. It is a CLI flag rather than a buried constant. Nobody has
  measured GC's real fill quality at these sizes.
- **One instrument, one month, one contract.** July 2026 GC. The √h scaling is a strong regularity
  and it is still 23 sessions of it.

### The decision this asks for

**Judge Stage 1's kill gate at 5 minutes**, and report 15 and 30 alongside it as descriptive
columns that are never the gate. Stage 2's regime survival already agreed independently — 92% at
5m, 78% at 15m, 54% at 30m — and it is worth saying that these are two unrelated arguments landing
on the same horizon. One is about whether a regime outlives the trade it selected; the other is
about whether the tape's noise leaves anything to measure. Neither knew about the other.

**This is a decision, not a finding, and it is not mine alone to take** — it changes what §6.1's
Part B thresholds are written *against*, and Part B is a pre-commitment, so it has to be settled
before the numbers go in rather than after.
