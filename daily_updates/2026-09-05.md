## Candle history is deeper than one call showed

Yesterday's `/kline` probe asked for the latest 500 daily candles, got 357, and stopped. That
number is not a history limit — it is 500 *calendar* days of which gold trades ~71%.
`query_kline_num` counts calendar slots, not bars.

Walking `kline_timestamp_end` backwards, per interval:

| Interval | Reaches back to | Bars per call | Span per call |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| 1-minute | **at least 2022-06-01** | 500 | 8.3 h |
| 15-minute | 2023-12 and earlier | ~408 | ~125 h |
| 1-hour | 2023-12 and earlier | ~350–411 | ~400 h |
| Daily | **2022-03-03**, and it genuinely ends there | ~357 | ~500 calendar days |

The daily walk terminated cleanly — four pages reached 2022-03-03 and a fifth ending at that date
returned a single bar, so that is the start of the series rather than a paging limit. The
1-minute series was probed at 2026-01, 2024-01 and 2022-06 and returned a full page each time,
so **intraday retention matches daily** rather than being trimmed to recent months.

That makes ~4.3 years of free 1-minute gold OHLCV a real dataset. It changes nothing in §1–§4 —
the bars still carry no order flow and their volume is still one CFD venue's — but it is the one
part of AllTick worth taking seriously.

## The only test that matters

`alltick/packages/edge/ohlcv_edge.py`. AllTick supplies no order flow, so anything built on it
comes out of OHLCV. If nothing survives on years of free bars, no tier is worth pricing and the
vendor question never arises.

Two parts, cheapest first.

**Part 1 is `horizon.py`'s diagnostic pointed at a different instrument** — does dispersion grow
as √h, and does the overlap correlation between adjacent bars' legs land on a random walk's
(h−b)/h. Measured on a 3,000-bar smoke sample (11 sessions, 5-minute):

```
        sigma  sigma/sqrt(h)  median|move|  overlap_rho  rw_predicted
 5m     40.92      1.000          19.9        -0.011        0.000
15m     70.69      0.997          35.3         0.658        0.667
30m     99.84      0.996          50.7         0.827        0.833
```

**AllTick spot gold is a random walk at these horizons, to within 0.4%** — the same result
`horizon.py` recorded for GC futures, from two directions that were not fitted to each other.
Whatever else the vendor difference is, it is not that one series has unconditional structure
the other lacks.

**Part 2 sweeps four OHLCV rules** — momentum, reversion, breakout, volume thrust — over a
threshold grid, each measured **through `backtest.evaluate`** so the leg semantics are the kill
gate's own. Because the whole grid is searched, the null is the **best-of-grid on shuffled
data**: forward moves permuted within session, which destroys the feature-to-outcome link while
preserving the feature distribution, the trade count and the side mix. Comparing a searched
grid to a single-draw null would find something by construction.

**The vectorised forward-move path is verified against the real harness rather than assumed
equivalent** — `max|diff| = 0.000000` and NaN-agreement `1.000` at all three horizons, on 298
sampled entries. The fast path is only used for the null; the reported rules go through
`backtest.evaluate`.

**No threshold in this file is committed.** The grid maps the space; where the pass line falls
is `thresholds_selector.md` §6.1's call and Varad's, not this file's. It reports and does not
decide.

### What the smoke run says, and what it cannot

On 3,000 bars the best-of-grid was `breakout k=1.0` at 9.47 ticks on n=35 — sitting **inside its
own null** (median 6.72, p95 14.51, 22% of shuffles beat it). That is a grid search finding the
grid.

**That number decides nothing.** 11 sessions is far below anything the design would act on, and
`n=35` is barely over `MIN_SAMPLES`. It demonstrates the pipeline runs end to end; the real
answer needs the full pull.

**The 5-minute pull to 2022-06 is running now** — ~610 pages, ~1.9 h, ~305,000 bars. The full
result goes in tomorrow's entry.

### Where this sits

If nothing survives on 4.3 years, AllTick is closed for good and no tier gets priced. If
something does, it is still spot/CFD OHLCV with a venue-specific volume, and the honest next
question is whether the same rule does better on GC's Databento bars — which carry real volume
and the order flow besides. **Either way this does not move C1.**

## The live signal pipeline, designed

Varad stated the product's core loop end to end, in his own words:

> When someone uses our extension — a Tauri floating window beside an MT5-like platform — and asks
> "is it buy or sell at that moment", the frontend reads the screen via OCR and passes it to the
> backend analysis, which identifies regime, ATR, CVD, order book, footprint. That goes to a bot
> model we train. The bot returns a signal with reasoning. The signal travels to a decision file
> where filters decide how many pips this trade will move. A feedback loop structures the JSON into
> human-readable form and the signal ships. **The goal: accurate trade signals with exactly how many
> pips it will travel, so that anyone can trust that many pips and place an order with huge lots.**

Written up as [`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-live-signal-pipeline-design.md`](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-live-signal-pipeline-design.md).
**Design only — nothing in it is built, and two of its six stages are blocked on things that do not
exist yet.** It takes no week from `plans/team/`, and says so, the same way the 25 Aug selector spec
did.

Two things in the brief changed. Nothing else did.

### OCR cannot supply CVD, order book or footprint — and S6 already said so

The brief routed all four numbers through the screen read. They cannot come from there: a rendered
candle threw the aggressor side away when it was drawn, and the screenshot of that candle threw it
away again. Spot gold has no centralised volume either — that is A2's finding from 25 Aug, and it is
why `real_volume` comes back empty on MT5.

`contracts.md` S6 froze this boundary and **named this exact request as the predicted failure**:
*"this is exactly the mistake that becomes tempting in Week 8 when a vision model returns something
that looks like volume and it would be so convenient."*

**The fix costs the product nothing it wanted.** Capture supplies context — symbol, timeframe, the
levels the trader drew, their actual question, what is on screen. The engine supplies every number.
They merge at the feature step, and the trader still gets an answer about the chart in front of them.
S6's forbidden-field rule is restated in the spec rather than amended, and the spec adds the case
that follows from it: **if the trader's chart is an instrument we have no feed for, the answer is "no
signal — no feed for XAUUSD"**, which is a UI state, not an error.

### "Exactly N pips, guaranteed" is not supportable — and the honest version is already in this repo

Two measurements, two instruments, two code paths not fitted to each other: `horizon.py` on GC and
`ohlcv_edge.py` on AllTick spot both landed on a **random walk at 5/15/30m, to within 0.4%**. A
random walk has no predictable magnitude, and a magnitude forecast is strictly harder than the
direction call it contains — its error bar is never smaller. `horizon.py` bounds it from the other
side too: an edge below a horizon's minimum detectable effect is not a small edge, it is an
unmeasurable one.

**The correct form is already written in `features/regime_filter.py`:** *the ATR floor is 40 ticks,
the volatility at which a 70-tick target becomes reachable inside a 6-bar window — P(move ≥ 70) is
45.8% in the 35-40 bucket and 55.3% in 40-45.* That is a pip forecast stated as a base rate, and a
trader can size on it because it repeats. `backtest.py`'s per-trade MFE/MAE — already signed in the
trade's own favour direction — is the raw material.

So the decision file emits a **distribution, not a scalar**: MFE p25/median/p75, MAE, and
P(reach target before stop) per candidate target, with the bucket and its `n` shown so the trader can
audit where the number came from.

### The S7 contract, enforced by absence

`PipForecast` + `Signal`, proposed and **not frozen** — freezing needs all three. It is enforced the
way S6 is, by what it has no field for: **no `guaranteedPips`, no lot size, no risk amount.**
`forecast: null` is a common and valid answer below `MIN_SAMPLES`; `suggested` is derived from the
reach table rather than chosen; `feed` is never omitted, because a stale feed invalidates everything
above it. No threshold in the stage has a default, for the reason `strategies.py` has none.

### The feedback loop, re-pointed

The brief had it formatting JSON. That is stage E, and it is the cheapest stage in the file. The
loop's real job is **calibration — when the model says 61%, does it happen 61% of the time.** That is
falsifiable weekly on live data without a backtest, it is the one claim a subscriber can verify
themselves, and it separates the two cases hit rate cannot: a model that is 55% accurate and says 55%
is useful; one that is 70% accurate and says 90% is a liability.

It reports and **does not auto-retrain** — a loop that chases last week leaves nobody able to say
which version produced which number.

### Two smaller things worth not losing

- **Pips are not ticks.** Every internal number is GC ticks (0.10, $10). MT5 "pips" on XAUUSD are
  broker-dependent, commonly 0.01 or 0.10. Conversion happens **once, at the display boundary,
  labelled**. Unconverted, a forecast is off by up to 10× — in the flattering direction.
- **Build order is F → B → A → C → D → E, not the diagram's order.** The outcome log goes first,
  before the first signal exists, because a calibration loop retrofitted later has no history. And
  §11's item 5: **the reach table alone is a complete Stage C+D with no model in it, and it should be
  built first and beaten** — a base-rate table that nothing outperforms is the correct product.

### What it does not resolve

Six open decisions, all Varad's, in §11 — Gate 1's three options for the empty signal set,
`MIN_SAMPLES`, the reach pass line, whether S7 freezes, what the model is, and **whether the pip
forecast ships to subscribers at all or stays a personal tool.** That last one is Shreyas's CA call:
a performance guarantee attached to a paid signal changes the answer to the SEBI Research Analyst
question `plans/current.md` still records as open.

## The reach table, built and measured

**Landed on `main` in `81de547`, with the calibration log below.**

The pipeline spec's §11 item 5 — *the reach table alone is a complete Stage C+D with no model in it,
and it should be built first and beaten.* Built today. **+107 lines in `backtest.py` (120 → 227),
9 tests, no new file and no new dependency.** Suite **131 green**, `ruff` and `mypy` clean.

### `first_touch` — the question MFE and MAE structurally cannot answer

A trade with `mfe = +50` and `mae = −30` either ran to its target or was stopped out on the way.
**Both outcomes produce the same two numbers**, because the order is the whole question and the order
is not in them. A hit rate read off MFE scores every stopped-out trade as a win.

`test_mfe_and_mae_cannot_tell_these_two_trades_apart_and_first_touch_can` is exactly that: two
hand-built frames, up-then-down against down-then-up, asserted to have identical MFE and MAE and
opposite `first_touch` against a 40/20 bracket.

### The same-bar ambiguity is reported, not asserted away

When one bar's high clears the target and its low clears the stop, the tape's order is not in the
bar. Default `ties="stop"` takes the worse reading, so `p_target` is a **lower** bound;
`reach_table` runs it both ways and reports `p_target` and `p_target_max`. **Never the midpoint** —
bars do not know where in that band the truth sits, and averaging would invent a precision.

`session_ends` was extracted rather than the leg rule re-derived in a second place — the same reason
`atr` moved into `features/expansion.py` on D2. `evaluate` now calls it.

### First real numbers

**July 2026, training half only** — 3,516 five-minute bars, 13 sessions, ≤ 2026-07-19. **The
held-out half was not touched.** Unconditional long entry on every bar, 30-minute horizon. This is
the base rate any signal has to beat.

```
 target  stop     n  p_target  p_target_max  p_stop  p_neither
     40    20  3438     0.282         0.329   0.691      0.027
     70    20  3438     0.164         0.179   0.756      0.080
     70    35  3438     0.225         0.232   0.593      0.182
    100    50  3438     0.144         0.146   0.462      0.393
```

By ATR bucket, at the 70/20 bracket the ATR floor was derived against:

| ATR | n | p_target | p_stop | EV |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| <30 | 415 | 0.108–0.108 | 0.728 | **−7.0 ticks** |
| 30–40 | 866 | 0.140–0.147 | 0.752 | **−5.3** |
| 40–50 | 870 | 0.155–0.160 | 0.772 | **−4.6** |
| 50+ | 1,222 | 0.213–0.247 | 0.762 | **−0.3** |

**No bucket clears zero.** That is the correct and expected shape — an unconditional entry on a
random walk should not pay, and `horizon.py` said the series is one. The value of the table is that
it is now the measured null, in the exact units the product would quote, rather than an assumption.

### One finding that corrects a stated mechanism

**The ATR gradient is real and monotone — `p_target` climbs 0.108 → 0.213 — but `p_stop` is flat at
0.73–0.77 across every bucket.** Higher volatility does not buy fewer stop-outs. It converts
*neither* into *target*: `p_neither` falls 0.164 → 0.025 while the stop rate does not move.

`regime_filter.py`'s ATR floor survives directionally; the reasoning offered for it does not.
**Nothing was tuned in response** — the floor is Part B's committed number and this is a measurement
against it, not a negotiation with it.

### The band is narrow where it matters

0.164–0.179 unconditionally, so the bar-resolution result is not an artefact of the tie rule. It
widens only in the 50+ bucket (0.213–0.247) — **exactly where `regime_filter.py` predicted a 20-tick
stop sits inside half a typical bar.** Tick resolution is still D4's job; this bounds how far it can
move the answer.

### What was deliberately not done

No threshold committed, no pass line, no bucketing scheme promoted, held-out half untouched. **No
conditional table yet** — that needs a signal set, which is Gate 1, and Gate 1 is still empty.

## The calibration log

`calibration.py` (`81de547`) — Stage F of the pipeline spec, and **step 1 of its build order, written before the
first signal exists.** That ordering is the point: a calibration loop retrofitted after the fact has
no history, and the first weeks are exactly when calibration is most informative. **152 lines, 11
tests, one new file, no new dependency.** Suite **142 green**, `ruff` and `mypy` clean.

The one question it answers: **when the model says 61%, does it happen 61% of the time.** Falsifiable
weekly on live data without a backtest, checkable by a subscriber themselves, and it separates the
two cases a hit rate cannot — a model that is 55% accurate and says 55% is a useful tool; one that is
70% accurate and says 90% is a liability.

### Three integrity properties, all structural rather than promised

**Append-only.** A signal record is never rewritten. An outcome is a *second* record joined by id, so
the forecast on disk stays byte-identical to the one the trader saw.
`test_settling_never_rewrites_the_forecast_that_was_shown` asserts exactly that on the raw line.
Calibration measured against retrospectively adjusted forecasts measures nothing at all, and the
adjustment would be invisible in the result — which is why this is a test and not a convention.

**`settle` cannot invent history.** It raises on an id that was never emitted, and on one already
settled. `read` raises on a hand-edited orphan outcome. Ids are generated rather than accepted, so no
caller can reuse one and quietly overwrite a forecast in the join.

**A forecast without its context is refused at the boundary.** `SIGNAL_FIELDS` requires `feed`,
`bucket`, `n` and `thresholds` alongside `p`. A probability off nine samples is not the same claim as
one off nine hundred, and a stale feed invalidates everything above it — so a row that cannot be
audited six months later is never written in the first place.

### A real bug the encoding test caught

`outcome` uses `backtest.first_touch`'s three-valued encoding (+1 target, −1 stop, 0 neither), not a
bool, because the live outcome and the historical base rate it gets compared against have to be the
*same* measurement rather than two that resemble each other.

The test for that failed on first run. **`True in (-1, 0, 1)` is `True` in Python** — bool subclasses
int — so a bool passed the guard, would have serialised as `true`, read back as a bool, and quietly
meant "hit". Now excluded with `type(outcome) is not int`, and the reason is in a comment because
nothing about that line is self-evident.

### Verified end to end on real data — and in-sample by construction

July training half, each ATR bucket's own base rate replayed as the forecast for every bar in it:

```
                 n  predicted  realised   thin
(0.0, 0.12]    415      0.108     0.108  False
(0.12, 0.15]   866      0.140     0.140  False
(0.15, 0.18]   870      0.155     0.155  False
(0.18, 0.25]  1222      0.213     0.213  False
```

**3,373 signals emitted, settled, read back, and `realised` equals `predicted` to three decimals in
all four bins.** That is what *must* happen — the forecast is the base rate of the same bars it is
scored on. **This tests the plumbing, not a model.** The held-out version is the real measurement and
it has not been run. Anyone reading this later should not take the table above as evidence of a
calibrated anything.

### One known limit, measured rather than guessed

`settle` reads the whole log, so a replay that settles N signals is **O(N²)** — 3,373 emit+settle
pairs took **26.5s**. Live use settles a handful a day against a log of hundreds, where that cost is
free, and both integrity guards above need that read to work at all. **Recorded in the docstring
rather than optimised away**; seeding the log from the full 19-month archive is the case that would
need batching, and nobody is doing that yet.

### What is not done

**Nothing is wired to it.** There is no producer, because Gate 1 is still empty and
`signals/engine.py` still fires zero signals. The log having no caller is the state it was
deliberately built to be ready for, not an oversight.

## Gate 1's option (a) is dead on arithmetic

The pipeline spec says run `horizon.py`'s power table **before** training, not after. The same logic
decides whether one of Gate 1's three options is worth taking at all — and one of them is not.

Small, **but it errs toward flattering the strategy, which is the direction that must not be free.**

## The click-through toggle, and the macOS focus bug that broke its first design

`apps/desktop` (`e0171bf`) — W1D3 per `phase-2-month-one.md`, Prathamesh's, first real behaviour
on the overlay rather than its shape. Row **B** in `plans/current.md`.

**Whole-window, not per-pixel.** The obvious design — track the cursor and flip pass-through
region by region so a live chart stays clickable while chrome around it doesn't — was tried first
and does not fit Tauri's API on this stack: `setIgnoreCursorEvents(true)` stops the OS from
delivering *any* mouse event to the window, `pointermove` included, so a window that just armed
pass-through goes deaf to the cursor at that exact moment and can never detect it moving back over
a button. There is no Electron-style `forward` option to fall back to (checked against
`@tauri-apps/api/window.d.ts` before writing anything). So the whole window arms and disarms at
once, from a toolbar button, rather than being inferred from pixel position.

### The macOS issue

The first disarm path was `Escape`, on the reasoning that keyboard focus and mouse pass-through
are independent OS properties — the window should keep hearing keystrokes even while it ignores
clicks. **True in principle, false in practice, found on-device on W1D3.** Once a pass-through
click landed on MT5 and focus came back via Cmd+Tab or the Dock icon, `Escape` went nowhere.
Cmd+Tab reliably reactivates the *app*; it does not reliably hand keyboard focus back to the
specific `WKWebView` inside a borderless, cursor-ignoring window. The result was a window armed
permanently — every click passing through, no way back short of killing the process. Exactly the
failure `phase-2-month-one.md` W2D2 already named in advance: "a hotkey that only works when our
window is already focused is not a hotkey."

The fix, pulled forward from W2D2: `Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+K`, registered at the **OS level** via
`tauri-plugin-global-shortcut` (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/lib.rs`), not as a `keydown` listener
inside the webview. It fires regardless of which app currently holds focus — MT5 included — and
calls `set_ignore_cursor_events(false)` directly on the Rust side, so a stuck or unresponsive
frontend still can't trap the window. `Escape` stays wired as a secondary path for when the
webview genuinely does hold focus; the hotkey is the one this is built to depend on. A second
on-device bug was caught the same day: the handler's first cut checked the pressed combo against
`Modifiers::SHIFT` alone, omitting Cmd/Ctrl, so it registered without error and silently never
fired — fixed by dropping the modifier check entirely, since exactly one shortcut is ever
registered and any call into the handler is unambiguously that one.

### What this means for Windows and Linux — untested, to do later

Everything above was found and fixed **on macOS only**. Nothing in it has run on Windows or
Linux, and none of it should be assumed to carry over silently:

- **The focus-loss bug is a `WKWebView` behaviour, not a proven cross-platform one.** Windows'
  Tauri backend is WebView2 (Chromium), a different engine with different focus semantics —
  Alt+Tab may return keyboard focus to it reliably where Cmd+Tab does not for `WKWebView`. This
  needs checking on-device rather than assumed fixed by the same code; the OS-level hotkey should
  stay regardless, but whether `Escape` is *also* usable on Windows is an open, testable question.
- **Linux's WebView backend (WebKitGTK) is WebKit-derived, same family as `WKWebView`.** The safer
  assumption is that the same focus-loss bug is present until a test says otherwise, not that
  Linux is fine because it isn't macOS.
- **`setIgnoreCursorEvents`'s all-or-nothing behaviour is only confirmed on macOS.** Whether
  WebView2 or WebKitGTK expose the same coarse-grained toggle, or something closer to Electron's
  per-region `forward`, is unchecked — worth a look before assuming the whole-window design is
  the only option on those platforms too.
- **The hotkey registration itself is unverified on Linux.** `CommandOrControl+Shift+K` is already
  written to map cross-platform (that's why it isn't hardcoded to `Cmd`), but global-shortcut
  registration under Wayland is a known trouble spot — some compositors don't grant it without
  portal support — and that risk is unmeasured here.
- Ties into `phase-2-month-one.md`'s existing gates: Week 4 wants a documented, unsigned Windows
  installer, and Week 8 has a separate cTrader compatibility pass ("window handle capture, overlay
  positioning, click-through") — this is the OS-level half of that same surface and should be
  checked alongside it rather than assumed done.

**Nothing above is scheduled yet** — there is no Windows or Linux dev machine in the loop right
now, so this is a recorded gap, not a task with an owner or a date.

## Four L1-only strategies — the analysis, and what it does to the regime document

Written at Varad's request, reading [`docs/strategy/xauusd-regimes.md`](../docs/strategy/xauusd-regimes.md)
against the measurements already in this repo. Output:
[`docs/strategy/four-l1-strategies.md`](../docs/strategy/four-l1-strategies.md).
**Nothing was built and no threshold was committed.** It is an analysis and a proposal.

### The blocker on the request as stated

The ask was four strategies that run on **both spot XAUUSD and GC**, from **L1 only**. Three of the
source document's four cannot be computed on spot gold at all: Strategy 2 needs VWAP and Strategy 3
needs `volume > 150% of average`, and spot gold has no centralised tape — **the same fact that cut
A2 on 25 Aug and closed AllTick on C2 this morning.** The two that survive the portability test,
trend-following and range-compression, are price-only, and price-only at 5/15/30m is what
`horizon.py` measured as a random walk to 0.1% and `ohlcv_edge.py` reproduced on spot to 0.4%.

So the honest cross-instrument feature set is **price, spread, quote rate and time.** Everything
else — delta, CVD, absorption, footprint, depth — is GC-only. That is a smaller surface than the
document assumes it is working on, and every strategy had to be re-specified onto it rather than
ported.

### The four proposed, and why only one is directional

**S1 bracket-geometry frontier** (non-directional) · **S2 compression → expansion** (magnitude, not
direction) · **S3 anchor-distance reversion** (directional) · **S4 clock and event conditioning**
(non-directional).

The split is not caution, it is where the evidence points. `FAMILIES.md` measured both arms onto
their own matched null — **−0.0058 and −0.0003** — at a power that would have caught a 30% relative
lift. `BASE_RATES.md` found the one thing that did replicate across 19 months and a regime change:
outcome distributions are ordered by volatility state, and the 50+ bucket held **0.2000 → 0.1989**
while the headline moved 2.2×. And `PipForecast` already sells a conditional distribution rather
than a side, so three strategies that sharpen the distribution are worth more here than three that
re-run an experiment that has already returned nothing.

**S1 is the one I would run first and it is not in the source document.** `base_rates.py` measured
one geometry — 70 ticks / 20 / 30 minutes — and it loses in all four ATR buckets. That is one point
on a surface nobody has swept. If a positive-EV geometry exists, every lift measured against
70/20/30m has been measured against the wrong null. It selects no entries, so like `BASE_RATES.md`
it costs no out-of-sample data, and it cannot fail — it returns a shape either way.

### Three architectural findings, each load-bearing

- **`s1.TICK = 0.10` is the whole coupling.** Every tick-denominated number in the repo flows through
  that line and `TICK_VALUE` beside it — `backtest`, `strategies`, `horizon`, `features/expansion`,
  `features/orderflow`. An MT5 broker may call a XAUUSD pip 0.01 or 0.10, a 10× spread in what "40"
  means, so **volatility buckets have to be redefined in basis points** before a GC base rate can
  legitimately be read as a prior for spot. This is the input-side twin of the pipeline design's
  §6.4 display trap, and it is the more dangerous of the two because it never renders wrong.
- **The committed regime labelling does not port.** `regimes.py` clusters on four features and two of
  them are order flow; the three regimes separate primarily on `cvd_slope` (+27.6 / −26.7 / −0.2) and
  `cvd_persistence` (0.61 / 0.61 / 0.21) — exactly what spot does not have. A portable regime needs a
  refit on the portable set and will be a different, probably weaker, labelling. It cannot be reached
  by relabelling and must not be assumed to transfer.
- **Quote rate is the only activity measure on both feeds, and it is vendor-relative.** AllTick
  delivered spot gold at 1 Hz and ~2% of ticks; Databento delivers every book update. A raw
  threshold on quote rate silently changes meaning when the vendor changes, so it enters as a
  trailing z-score within instrument and vendor. Same class of error as July's `p_stop` looking flat.

Two smaller ones worth recording: **cost is higher on spot, not lower** (15–30 cents of broker spread
against `horizon.py`'s 1.4-tick GC floor, and it widens on exactly the events S4 cares about) — but
spread is an L1 observable, so `cost_ticks` can stop being a scalar and become a per-bar series. And
order flow should be a **veto layer only** on GC: it may suppress a signal, never create one and
never flip a side, or the two instruments are running different strategies under one name and no
cross-instrument comparison means anything.

### What was cut from the source document, and why

Pattern recognition (§3) — 419 sessions is ~2,500 H4 bars, and a pattern firing 30–40 times cannot be
adjudicated against `horizon.py`'s MDE math. Not wrong; unmeasurable. **MODWT wavelet denoising (§4A)
as described is acausal** — zeroing high-frequency components and reconstructing means the denoised
price at every bar already knows where price went next, which destroys a backtest silently and
flatteringly. Kalman is causal and fine but is an adaptive EMA — smoothing, not information. And
Strategy 1's citation of Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen is a **1–12 month holding-period result** being
used to justify a 5-minute EMA cross, three orders of magnitude from where the finding was made.

The macro table (§5) is kept, demoted from signal to slow conditioner plus an event calendar — which
is S4, and S4 needs no market data at all.

### Order of work, and the one thing it is designed to avoid

**Steps 2–4 — the geometry sweep, the clock profile, the compression test — need no new data, no
feed and no vendor decision.** They run against the 46M trades already on disk. That ordering is the
point: if S1, S2 and S4 all come back flat, it is worth knowing *before* the spot-feed question is
answered, not after money is spent on it.
