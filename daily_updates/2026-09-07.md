## The surface is zero minus cost

3,456 cells — 72 grid points × 4 `atr_bp` buckets × 6 phases × 2 sides — over 19 months, both sides,
1h 35m, matching the projection to within 10%. Every cell has n ≥ 400.

| | ATR per leg |
| :--- | ---: |
| mean gross EV, **before cost** | **−0.0032** |
| mean cost (1.4 ticks round trip) | **+0.0423** |
| mean net EV | **−0.0455** |

**Gross EV is zero to three decimal places and the entire negative result is the commission.** It
does not move: −0.0037 / −0.0033 / −0.0026 by horizon, +0.0007 to −0.0077 by bucket. Seventy-two
brackets, six phases, both sides, sixteen months of tape, and no corner of it pays for a round trip.

That is the benchmark `test_m3_ev.py` names — *EV on a driftless random walk is about zero* — and it
is the third independent confirmation of the same fact, after `horizon.py` on GC and `ohlcv_edge.py`
on spot to within 0.4%.

## 159 cells pass, and none is a finding

Reported before the interpretation, because the pass line was committed and is not being moved.
**159 of 3,456 (4.6%) clear all three conditions.**

**The two tools that disqualify them were both committed before the run**, which is the only reason
this reads as analysis rather than as excuse-making:

- **The side mirror removes 145.** Only 68 of 3,456 cells are positive once the opposite side is
  averaged in. A cell that pays long and loses short is the archive's direction wearing a bracket's
  clothes.
- **The barrier/open split removes 13 of the remaining 14.** They lose money on trades that
  *closed* and get their entire EV from mark-to-market on legs still open at the horizon — a
  selected set, biased toward the target by construction. Mean over the 159: `ev_barrier` −0.0802
  against `ev_open` +0.1420.

What survives both is **one cell**: `(7,10] bp · London-NY · long · 3.0×/0.75× · 60m`, at
**`ev_sym` +0.0005 ATR — two hundredths of a tick** — whose own mirror fails.

**351 cells (10.2%) are undecided by the same-bar tie band** and none of them passes. They stay
undecided until `replay.py`, which is the tie-resolution argument standing exactly where
Prathamesh's `008c61f` put it.

## The passes are M3's artefact

`London-NY` is **17% of the surface and 73% of the passing cells.**

Same phase `M3_CLOCK.md` §2 diagnosed, same mechanism: `atr_bp` looks backwards 60 minutes, so at
08:00 ET the bracket is sized off quiet late-London and is too small for the 08:30 release and 09:30
open that follow. The signature is here too — **`London-NY`'s mean `p_neither` is 0.164 against
0.264 surface-wide.** A bracket that resolves more often, measured against a null pooled over phases
that resolve less often, shows a `p_target` lift. **M1 is rediscovering M3's volatility seasonality,
which M3 already showed converts to no EV.**

**On contiguity, held to the pre-committed standard.** §1 said a lone grid point is an artefact but
*"a contiguous region clearing it is a finding"*. The passes **are** contiguous — `London-NY` long
in `(7,10]` passes a solid block at `target ≥ 3.0×` across all three horizons. **That test is met
and it does not rescue the result**, because the block sits exactly where `ev_barrier` is most
negative and `ev_open` largest. The short side's block sits in the opposite corner of the grid,
which is not what a geometry premium looks like.

## The prediction, scored

- *"No cell clears the cost floor"* — **wrong on the letter.** 159 do.
- *"If one does, expect it at targets ≤ 1.0×"* — **wrong, and in the opposite direction.** The
  passes concentrate at 3.0× and 4.0×: a *low* `p_target` at a wide target leaves most legs
  unresolved, and the mark-to-market on those carries the EV.
- *The mechanism — random walk, nothing to pay for cost* — **right, and it is the finding.**

**Wrong conclusion, right mechanism.** The error was assuming a random walk implies no cell clears
an EV screen. It does not: on 3,456 cells, drift and open marks produce passes at exactly this rate,
and the pre-committed mirror and split are what tell the two apart. That lesson is worth more than
having been right.

## What it does not do

**It does not kill the vol lane.** `strategy-split.md` §2's amended line requires no positive-EV
cell **and** M2 inside its own MDE. Cells cleared. **M1 does not fire the kill condition on the
letter and it is not being reinterpreted to make it fire.** M2 is owed, and M1's surface is now the
corrected null it gets quoted against — which is what step 2 existed to produce.

It also does not spend the held-out half: nothing selected, tuned or fitted, so by `BASE_RATES.md`'s
reasoning it cost no out-of-sample data. **One cell at +0.0005 ATR is not worth spending it on.**

## M2 clears — the first thing in the track that does

Step 4, run on the **training half only** (2025-01…09, per §6's committed `SPLIT`). Thresholds from
[`strategy-precommit.md`](../plans/team/strategy-precommit.md) §8, committed and pushed with a git
timestamp earlier than the driver existed. Result:
[`analysis/M2_MAGNITUDE.md`](../services/signal-data/analysis/M2_MAGNITUDE.md).

**An EXPANDING volatility state raises the probability that price travels a given multiple of its
own ATR.**

| T | H | CONTRACTING | STABLE | EXPANDING | relative |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1.0 | 60 | 0.6243 | 0.6266 | **0.6399** | +2.5% |
| 1.5 | 15 | 0.1642 | 0.1690 | **0.1778** | **+8.3%** |
| 2.0 | 15 | 0.0789 | 0.0856 | **0.0898** | **+13.8%** |

**EXPANDING beats CONTRACTING in 9 of 9 (T, H) combinations and STABLE in 9 of 9.** Nine sign tests
landing the same way is a 1-in-512 event under a coin flip. **17 of 36 cells clear their own
`mde_rate`.**

### The comparison that makes it different in kind from M1

**M1 passed 4.6% of 3,456 cells, which is what noise produces. M2 passes 47% of its 36.** Same
`mde_rate`, same bucket edges, same archive, same power arithmetic. That contrast is the strongest
single argument that this one is real.

### It is scale, not direction

```
EXPANDING     P(move >= +T) 0.1816   P(move <= -T) 0.1578    gap +0.0238
CONTRACTING   P(move >= +T) 0.1527   P(move <= -T) 0.1557    gap -0.0030
```

Both tails rise together — the gap is 7.5% of the hit rate. **So M2 does not contradict
`horizon.py`'s random walk, it extends it:** only direction was ever tested, and a series can be
unpredictable in direction while being predictable in scale.

**⚠️ One residue, flagged rather than smoothed over.** EXPANDING carries a **+0.024 up-tilt that
CONTRACTING does not.** Gold rose across the archive, so vol expansions may simply co-occur with
up-moves — but that is a hypothesis. M2 claims no direction and this is not being read as one. It is
the first thing to ask the held-out half.

### Where it fails is informative

| bucket | mean lift | cells clearing MDE |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| `<7 bp` | +0.0169 | 3 of 9 |
| `7–10 bp` | **+0.0337** | **9 of 9** |
| `10–14 bp` | +0.0292 | 5 of 9 |
| `14+ bp` | **+0.0066** | **0 of 9** |

**The information sits in the middle of the volatility distribution and vanishes at the top.**
`atr_bp` already conditions on the level, so in the loudest bucket the bucket has done the slope's
work. **That is the measurement of how much `vol_state` and `atr_bp` overlap** — completely there,
not at all in `7–10` — and it is what earns `vol_state` a real second dimension in `reach.py`'s key.

### Size, said plainly

**Mean lift +0.033 over the passing cells, +0.022 over all 36.** Two to five points of probability:
a real conditioner, not a large one, and exactly what §4.4 asked M2 to be.

### The prediction, scored

- *"Clears its MDE at all three T and all three H"* — **right that it clears, wrong on where.** Not
  the `14+` bucket, which clears nothing.
- *"Largest lift at 60 minutes"* — **wrong.** True at `T=1.0`; at `T=1.5` and `2.0` the largest
  relative lift is at **15 minutes**. Vol clustering shows up fastest in the tail, the opposite of
  what I wrote.
- *"Pure scale, no directional content"* — **right in the main, wrong in the residue** above.
- *"It will not convert"* — **untested here by construction.** M2 has no bracket.

**Better calibrated than M1, and the same correction applies to both: mechanism right, location
wrong.**

### What it does not do

**It does not make anything tradeable.** M2's output is `P(|move| ≥ T)`, not a trade. The moment it
becomes a bracket it meets M1's surface, where gross EV is −0.0032 ATR against a cost of +0.0423,
and **nothing here has measured whether a +0.033 lift is worth more than that gap.** The natural
next run is M1's sweep conditioned on EXPANDING — which spends more out-of-sample data and should be
a decision rather than a drift.

**And the vol lane is alive.** `strategy-split.md` §2 kills it only if M1 finds no positive-EV cell
**and** M2 lands inside its MDE. Neither happened. **The held-out half (2025-10…2026-07) is
unspent.**

### Also: the three features, and one departure recorded

`rv_parkinson`, `rv_slope` and `efficiency_ratio` are filled — the last three stubs in Varad's half
of `features/portable.py` — with 12 tests against the zero-denominator cases, where the natural
fallback is a number that ranks the deadest window of the month as its cleanest signal.

**`rv_slope` returns the RELATIVE change, not the raw bp change its stub proposed.** A +2 bp move in
vol is an enormous expansion in a 5 bp regime and noise in a 20 bp one, and this archive's median
`atr_bp` runs 5.66 to 17.04 across its months — so a fixed bp cut would mean two different things at
its two ends. The tests measure what the relative form is *not* scale-free against: `ln(H/L)` is
linear in the range only to first order, worth 1.3e-4 at a 4% bar, four orders of magnitude below
what M2 looks for.

`m2_magnitude.surface()` has 9 tests. **Written after the surface was read, unlike
`test_m1_sweep.py`'s — a weaker guarantee, and labelled as one in the file.**


## And it cannot be harvested by a bracket

`M2_MAGNITUDE.md` §6 named this run and said it *"spends more out-of-sample data and should be
decided rather than drifted into"*. **Varad decided it**, so it was pre-committed
([`strategy-precommit.md`](../plans/team/strategy-precommit.md) §9, pushed in `2c781c2` before the
driver changed) and run on the **training half only** — the choice to condition on EXPANDING was
itself made from a training-half result, so running all 19 months would have pushed that choice into
the held-out data.

Result: [`analysis/M1_EXPANDING.md`](../services/signal-data/analysis/M1_EXPANDING.md). 1,728 cells,
both sides, every cell n ≥ 400.

**Conditioning adds +0.004 ATR per leg. The cost floor is +0.0423. Short by a factor of ten.**

| | mean `ev_delta` | mean `ev_lo` |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| CONTRACTING | −0.0006 | −0.0524 |
| STABLE | −0.0028 | −0.0546 |
| **EXPANDING** | **+0.0040** | **−0.0479** |
| all 1,728 cells | +0.0002 | |

EXPANDING is genuinely the best of the three states — and best by one tenth of what a round trip
costs. **The committed falsification condition returned zero cells.**

### The mechanism, measured rather than argued

| | `p_target` | `p_stop` | `p_neither` |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **EXPANDING − CONTRACTING** | **+0.0103** | **+0.0162** | −0.0263 |

**Both barriers get hit more, and the stop gets hit more than the target.** That is what "scale, not
direction" costs when a bracket is attached to it: the extra movement has no preferred sign, so it
distributes in proportion to how near each barrier is — and **the stop is the nearer one at almost
every point on the grid** (stops 0.5–1.5× ATR, targets 0.75–4.0×).

The arithmetic reconciles at the grid's mean geometry:

```
dEV = T x dp_target - S x dp_stop = 2.04 x (+0.0103) - 0.94 x (+0.0162) = +0.0058 ATR
```

against the observed +0.0040, the remainder being `ev_open` as `p_neither` falls. **M2's +0.033
probability lift converts to +0.004 ATR, and the conversion factor is the geometry.**

**This is the same fact `M3_CLOCK.md` §2 found from the other side** — `London-NY` had the highest
`p_target` on the board and the worst EV because `p_stop` rose with it. M3 found it in the clock;
this finds it in the vol state. **Two conditioners, one mechanism.**

### The 113 cells above the cost floor are a variance tail

6.5% of cells clear +0.0423 — until you look at the mirror: **121 cells (7.0%) sit symmetrically
below −0.0423, and both tails are the same 101 CONTRACTING cells** at n ≈ 850, the thinnest in the
run. Dispersion, not signal. Of the 13 cells passing §1's line, 5 survive the side mirror and **none
of those makes money on trades that closed** (mean `ev_barrier` −0.1437 against `ev_open` +0.2088).

### The prediction, scored

- *"`ev_delta` ≈ 0, no cell clears symmetrically"* — **right.**
- *"Both barriers get hit more, so EV is unchanged"* — **right, and sharpened**: the stop is hit
  *more* than the target, so conditioning is mildly **adverse** on the barrier side.

**The first prediction in the track right on both conclusion and mechanism.** M1's was wrong twice
with the mechanism right; M2's was right that it clears and wrong about where. The difference is
that this one was derived from two prior measurements rather than from a prior.

### What it settles

> **A magnitude edge with no directional content cannot be harvested by a symmetric bracket.** It is
> real — M2 measured it 9 of 9 — and it belongs in a forecast, not in a trade.

That is what `PipForecast` was designed to sell. §4.4 asked M2 to be *"a second conditioning
dimension for stage 7"*, and this confirms that is the **only** thing it can be. `vol_state` earns
its place in `reach.py`'s key on M2's evidence and **forfeits any claim to being an entry filter** on
this one.

**Not settled:** whether an *asymmetric* exit could harvest it. That is M4's question, blocked on
`replay.py` rather than on data. And the held-out half is still unspent, now carrying two questions
rather than one.

# Route 2, step one — Dukascopy works, and it was never a reachability problem

`analysis/SPOT_FEED_CHECK.md`. ARCHITECTURE §4.1 asked for *"a ten-minute check that a download
completes"* before anything else in Route 2. It passes.

**One hour, 2025-06-18 14:00 UTC, London–NY overlap:** 20,654 quotes, 344/min, mid 3373.861 …
3392.471, **spread median 1.91 bp** (p10 1.64, p90 2.15, max 3.77), fetch and decode in 1.2 s warm.

**The finding is not "it works" — it is why it looked like it didn't.** A request without a browser
`User-Agent` is **reset, not refused**: no status code, no body, a ~25-second hang and then
`Connection reset by peer`. That is indistinguishable from an unreachable host, and it is very
likely what `DELTA_CVD_FINDINGS.md` §4 recorded as "unreachable". With the header the identical URL
returns 200. Diagnosed with controls rather than assumed — `github.com` 200 and
`www.dukascopy.com` 302 from the same shell in the same second.

**A negative infrastructure result that was never differential-diagnosed sat in this repo and gated
a whole route of the strategy track.** One control request separates "host is down", "we are
firewalled" and "the host dislikes our headers", and those have completely different costs.

**Cross-checked against our own archive, same hour.** GC futures 3391.70 … 3411.50 against spot
3373.86 … 3392.47 — a **+19 basis, which is cost of carry**, not the 10× mismatch a guessed point
scale would produce. `dukascopy.POINTS` carries the scale explicitly and refuses an instrument it
has no scale for, which is §4.6 applied at the vendor boundary.

**What it closes and what it does not.** §9's open question 3 is answered. §7's doubted precondition
— *is spot spread degenerate like GC's?* — is confirmed **non-degenerate**, so a trailing z-score
has something to work with. **That is the precondition, not the result:** one hour, one session, one
broker says nothing about whether spread predicts anything, and §7's caveat stands — spot spread is
a broker pricing decision, never a threshold that transfers.

**What a bulk pull costs, measured rather than assumed.** 1.2 s warm, but under sequential load:
10.0 s, 11.2 s, **reset**, 21.6 s — **one request in four failed with the User-Agent set**, and as a
reset rather than an HTTP status, so `raise_for_status` never sees it. 19 months is ~10,000 hourly
files. **Not a ten-minute job**; it needs a resumable script that verifies what it already has —
the GC restore on 6 Sep is the cautionary case, where `fetch_or_load` reused a cache file without
validating it and only the manifest's `sha256` caught the risk.

**Two traps recorded so nobody pays twice.** The month in the URL is **zero-indexed** — June 2025 is
`/2025/05/`, and an off-by-one returns a *real file for the wrong month* that decodes and looks
fine. And an hour with no ticks is a **zero-byte file, not a 404**; Saturday returns 0 quotes in
0.8 s, and `decode_bi5` returns an empty frame rather than raising, because on a 24×5 instrument the
gap is the normal case.

`dukascopy.py` + `tests/test_dukascopy.py`, 7 tests, **none of which touch the network** — a suite
that fails when a vendor is briefly down is a suite people learn to ignore. **255 green.**
