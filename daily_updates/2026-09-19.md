## 10. ⛔ What this does NOT unblock, and the number that decides it

**No bars were pulled and no Track C step ran.** Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters.

**(1) Arithmetic. HTTP is a bounded-window transport, not an archive transport.** ~3–5 minutes a
day against S3's one object: a month is an hour, and **the walk-forward's five years is ~1,776
trading days ≈ 120 hours**. S3 remains the bulk path at ~$0.12 and **the credential task is not
retired by any of this** — it is now worth ten minutes instead of blocking a layer.

**(2) The feed degraded under exactly the use this was built for, and the attempt is recorded
rather than the intention.** A real `spot.pull` of 2026-08 over HTTP **aborted on the first day**:
2026-08-01 is a Saturday and hour 0 returned 503 through all five attempts. **503 is overloaded —
it is returned both by the throttle and, apparently, by hours the market was shut** — and the two
cannot be told apart from the status code. The probe written to separate them (rest 90s, then a
known-good hour, a Saturday hour, and a second known-good hour) **never completed its first
request**: after ~120 requests the feed stopped answering rather than refusing. That is E0's
finding in a new costume, and it is why nothing here claims the HTTP path is a solved transport.

**`fetch_day` does not and will not treat a 503 as an empty hour.** It would make weekend days pull
cleanly and it would also, on a throttled weekday, write a day with a hole that reads as a calm
session forever after. **Guessing which 503 is which is the one thing this module must not do.**
The fix is §11's: state the week as a fact about the instrument instead.

## 11. The trading week, stated rather than inferred — `SHUT` and `BOUNDARY`

**Varad's call, 19 Sep: take the HTTP bounded window, and solve the weekend at the pull level.**
So `dukascopy.py` now carries which UTC hours the feed has no object for, by weekday, and
`fetch_day` reads the week instead of reading a status code.

**The DST problem, and why the table is two tables.** `instruments.XAUUSD` records the week as
"~22:00 Sun to 21:00 Fri UTC", and the tilde is the whole difficulty: under EDT the week runs
22:00 Sun → 21:00 Fri with a 21:00–22:00 daily break, and under EST every boundary moves an hour
later. A DST-naive table would skip hours that hold data on half the calendar.

- **`SHUT` is the *intersection* of the two regimes** — Saturday entire, Sunday 00:00–21:59, Friday
  22:00–23:59. These hours have no object under either, so they are **never requested at all**.
  **A weekend day now costs zero requests**, which is both the fix and a ~28% cut in the requests
  a month needs.
- **`BOUNDARY` is where the regimes disagree** — the daily break (21:00 or 22:00) and the two
  weekly boundaries themselves. There and *only* there, a persistent 503 is accepted as "no
  object". **Everywhere else it still aborts the day unwritten.** At most two hours a day.

**The invariant is intact where it matters.** Every hour that must hold data under both regimes is
still fetched, and still aborts the day if it does not arrive. What was relaxed is exactly the set
of hours where absence is a legitimate answer — and that set is enumerated in the source, not
inferred at runtime.

**Three tests, and one of them is the pair's shape:** a Saturday makes no request at all (the
`AssertionError` fires if it does); a dead 21:00 on a Thursday yields a 23-hour day while a dead
14:00 on the same Thursday raises; and no hour appears in both tables, since an hour in both would
be skipped without ever being asked for. **668 passing, +8 on the session.**

⚠️ **Owed, and not yet done: verifying the week against the archive it just pulled.** The table is
written from the venue's published hours and the repo's own prose, not measured. The first full
month on disk can check it — the first and last tick of each week, and where the daily gap
actually falls — and until that runs, `SHUT` is an assumption with tests around its shape rather
than a measurement.

## 14. §10's stop band moved from dollars to ATR multiples — Varad's call, and what it did

**§13b's finding, acted on.** §10's `[$2, $5]` is 20–50 ticks at GC's $0.10 tick: a fact about GC in
2025, not about a stop. On spot at ~$4,175 it refused ~90% of what reached it while its floor
refused nothing at all.

**The conversion is measured, not chosen.** Six months of 2025 GC — 34,956 5-minute bars, through
the same `regime.atr_usd` Track C runs on spot — give a **median 60-minute ATR of $2.56**. So §10's
own numbers are **$2.00 / 2.56 = 0.78× ATR** and **$5.00 / 2.56 = 1.95× ATR**. Rounded:
`D_MIN_ATR = 0.8`, `D_MAX_ATR = 2.0`. **On GC these reproduce $2.05–$5.12 — §10 restated, not
loosened.** A test asserts exactly that, so the conversion cannot drift into a new risk appetite.

**Where it lives.** `fsm.D_MIN_ATR` / `D_MAX_ATR` replace the USD pair, `fsm.ROW` and `fsm_row()`
gain `atr_usd` — passed explicitly like `d_usd` and `slippage_usd`, so **the S13 seam is untouched
and this needed no second lane** — and `config/track_c.toml` + `track_c/bracket.py` follow. The
`_agree` cross-check still pins the TOML to `fsm.py`, so the two cannot drift.

**`no_atr` is a new refusal, and it is a NaN guard.** `NaN < x` and `NaN > x` are both False, so an
absent ATR would have cleared the floor *and* the cap and admitted a stop nothing checked — the
same shape as the NaN score that once passed `admit`. It sits at **0 in every column** of the run
below, which is the expected reading: each strategy's `inputs` stage already refuses a NaN ATR.

### ⚠️ A consequence worth stating: §10 no longer binds s1 at all

s1's stop is `level − 1.00 × ATR`, its entry `close − 0.25 × ATR`, `not_extended` caps the
extension at `0.75 × ATR`, and `_broken_level` requires the close to clear by §9's
`delta = 0.05 × ATR`. So

    D = extension + 0.75 ATR,   0.05 ATR <= extension <= 0.75 ATR   =>   D in [0.80, 1.50] x ATR

**That is inside `[0.8, 2.0]` at both ends** — s1's minimum is *exactly* the new floor. Its geometry
was already ATR-scaled, so an ATR-scaled bound agrees with it by construction and can never fire.
Correct rather than broken, and pinned as a test so a future reader does not diagnose a dead gate.

## 15. 🔴 `h_agree` is measuring the estimators, not the market — and production sits inside a bias the repo had already written a test about

**With §10 no longer masking it, `hurst_agree` is the largest cut in every funnel** — it refuses
roughly three bars in four for all four strategies. Checked, and it is not a market property.

### The arithmetic first

`hurst.SCALES` declares seven scales up to 256. A scale survives only if the window holds four of
it, measured against `window − 1` because DFA differences its input. At `hurst_window_bars = 256`:

    reachable scales = (4, 8, 16, 32)     four of seven, largest scale 32

**That is exactly the ladder `test_dfa_is_biased_high_on_a_short_scale_ladder_and_agreement_fails_
there` was written about** — it restricts a pure random walk to `(4, 8, 16, 32)` and records DFA
reading **0.563 against a true 0.5**. The bias was documented in a test on 18 Sep and then
configured into production.

⚠️ **The config comment was also wrong and is corrected**: it read *"§6's estimators need 4 windows
at the largest scale; 256 gives them"*. It does not — 256 gives four windows at scale **32**. Four
windows at the largest declared scale needs **1025**.

### The control that settles it

300 synthetic random walks, true `H = 0.5` by construction, at the production window:

| window | DFA | variance-time | gap | agree |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **256** | **0.562** | **0.420** | **+0.142** | **16.7%** |
| 512 | 0.538 | 0.438 | +0.100 | 27.3% |
| 1024 | 0.528 | 0.451 | +0.078 | 29.0% |
| 4096 | 0.524 | 0.489 | +0.035 | 67.0% |

**On data whose answer is known, and which both estimators should read as 0.5, they disagree by
+0.142 against a 0.05 tolerance and agree 16.7% of the time.** The real archive agrees on **26.2%**
of bars — *better* than the synthetic floor. A gate that a random walk fails more often than the
market does is not measuring the market.

### On the real archive

June–August 2026, 17,865 bars with both estimates:

| window | scales used | bias (dfa−vt) | sd | agree |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **256 (production)** | 4 of 7 | **+0.084** | 0.118 | **26.2%** |
| 512 | 5 of 7 | +0.058 | 0.074 | 35.1% |
| 1024 | 6 of 7 | +0.047 | 0.052 | 50.0% |
| 2048 | 7 of 7 | +0.023 | 0.036 | 72.8% |

**DFA reads above variance-time on 77.4% of bars** — a systematic offset, not noise. And the offset
alone (+0.084) **exceeds the tolerance it is tested against** (0.05), so the two estimators cannot
agree on a typical bar by construction. Removing the median offset lifts agreement only to 33.3%,
so there is large noise on top: sd 0.118, more than twice the tolerance.

### ⛔ Not changed, and deliberately

`hurst_window_bars` is a §6 threshold, and moving it **after** seeing its effect on a funnel is
precisely what §7's gates exist to prevent. Unlike §10's ATR conversion — which came from GC and
could have been derived before a spot bar existed — there is no such external anchor here: the honest
description of "raise the window until agreement improves" is tuning.

**What is recorded instead** is the arithmetic, as
`test_the_production_window_reaches_only_five_of_the_seven_declared_scales`, which pins the
reachable ladder and derives 1025 from the rule rather than from a result. It fails the day the
window moves, which is when the room should be looking at it.

**The choice is the room's, and it is a real one:**

1. **Window 1025** — reaches the whole declared ladder and is the only value consistent with
   `SCALES` as written. Costs ~3.5 days of 5-minute lookback per estimate.
2. **Shorten `SCALES`** to what 256 can actually reach, and accept DFA's bias at that ladder.
3. **Widen `hurst_agree_tol`** above the measured offset — but a tolerance chosen to exceed a known
   bias is not an agreement test.
4. **Drop `h_agree` as a gate** and read the estimates directly, against §6's "do not trade from one
   noisy Hurst estimate".

## 16. ⭐ `hurst_window_bars` 256 → 1025 — Varad's call, option 1, and the residual it leaves

**The value is derived, not chosen, and that is the entire argument for it.** A scale survives only
if the window holds four of it against `window − 1` (DFA differences its input), and `SCALES` tops
out at 256, so the smallest window reaching the whole declared ladder is **`4 × 256 + 1 = 1025`**.
Any smaller number silently truncates the ladder — and a truncated ladder is not a cheaper estimate
of the same quantity, because H is scale-dependent.

**This is a consistency fix rather than a tuning, and the distinction is load-bearing.** 1025
follows from `SCALES` and the four-window rule alone. It could have been derived before a single bar
was pulled, and it is the *only* value that makes the two constants agree. That it also improves
agreement is a **consequence, not the reason** — which matters, because §7's gates exist to stop
thresholds drifting toward a nicer funnel. The test is written against the rule (`4 × max(SCALES) +
1`) rather than against 1025, so it survives a change to `SCALES` and fails if the window drops back
under it.

### What it did to the estimators

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| scales reached | 4 of 7 (max 32) | **7 of 7 (max 256)** |

| bias (dfa − vt) | +0.084 | **+0.054** |
| sd of the difference | 0.118 | **0.057** |
| median `h_dfa` | 0.536 | **0.512** |

**The noise halved and DFA moved toward 0.5**, which is what removing a finite-size bias should
look like.

### 🔴 The residual, stated plainly

**A +0.054 bias still exceeds the 0.05 tolerance**, and DFA still reads above variance-time on
**80.3%** of bars. So agreement is 44%, not ~100%: **more than half of all bars still refuse on
`h_agree`, and that refusal is now an estimator property rather than a configuration error.**

Option 1 fixed what was demonstrably broken — a ladder the config claimed to use and did not. It did
**not** make the two estimators agree, and nothing in the four options would have, short of widening

## 17. 🔴 Why s4 produces zero suggestions: its geometry cannot satisfy §10, and never could

**Not a threshold slightly out of range — the arithmetic does not close on any bar in the archive.**

s4 breaks a Donchian edge and hides its stop behind **the opposite** edge, deliberately: *"the
structure being broken is the whole band"*. So

    D = (close − far edge) + (stop_pad − entry_pullback) × ATR  >  channel width + 0.50 × ATR

**The channel is 20 bars — 100 minutes — and the ATR is 60 minutes.** A max-minus-min over the
longer horizon runs several times the average true range over the shorter one, so the channel width
dominates D and §10's cap ends up measured against the wrong quantity.

Measured over June–August 2026, 17,638 bars:

| width / ATR | min | p1 | median | max |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| | **1.59** | 2.45 | **4.48** | 23.44 |

**The narrowest channel in three months gives `D = 2.09 × ATR` against a cap of `2.00`.** Not the
median bar — *the single best one in 17,638*, and it still misses. At the median, D is about
**5 × ATR**, two and a half times the cap. **0.00% of bars qualify.**

**It was equally impossible under the old dollar band**: at the median ATR of $4.53 the median D is
about **$23 against a $5.00 cap**. So s4 has never been able to fire, under either regime — which is
why §10's move to ATR multiples lifted every other strategy (s1 17→38, s2 25→57, s3 0→4) and left
this one at exactly zero. **The "control" reading in §14 was right that the bound still fires on s4;
what it missed is that for s4 the bound can *only* fire.**

**Same class of error as `TRACK_C_BUILD.md` §3(2)** — s1's `range_width` comparing a six-hour Asian
range against a one-hour ATR — and invisible for the same reason: nobody had bars.

⚠️ **A claim in `config/track_c.toml` is falsified and has been corrected.** The note under the four
`stop_pad_atr` values said the sizes "were also chosen so that a structural stop LANDS inside §10's
`[$2, $5]` band at XAUUSD's typical 60-minute ATR". **For s4 that is false and always was.** The
other three do land inside; only s4's cannot.

### ⛔ Not fixed, because every repair is a strategy decision

1. **Stop behind the *broken* edge** — `D = 0.50 × ATR + delta`, always inside the band. But s4's own
   docstring rejects exactly this: *"a stop just under the broken edge is a stop inside the noise
   that produced the squeeze."*
2. **Give s4 its own cap near `5 × ATR`** — then its risk per trade is 2.5× the other three, and §10
   stops being one rule.
3. **Shorten the Donchian** — but `bb_bars = 20` is Bollinger's default and the config says it is
   deliberately not tuned.
4. **Kill s4 and print the reason** — which is what `TRACK_C_BUILD.md` §7 prescribes for a strategy
   that fails, and the only option that costs nothing elsewhere.

Pinned as `test_s4_cannot_satisfy_section_10_with_a_stop_behind_the_far_edge`, which asserts the

### The smaller ones

- **Refresh race** — `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the token row. Two concurrent refreshes on one
  token now serialise; the second sees `used_at` set and trips reuse detection instead of both
  succeeding.
- **Uploads typed by their bytes.** `payments.sniff()` reads the PNG/JPEG/WebP/PDF signatures
  and that — not the client's `Content-Type` — decides the stored extension and the served type.
  An HTML file labelled `image/png` is a 415. The test also sends a PDF labelled `image/png` and
  checks it comes back as `application/pdf`. The existing payment test's fake PNG (`\x89PNG fake`)
  had to become a real header, which is the check working.
- **N+1 on `/admin/users`.** `UserFacts` gathers active keys, approver emails, payment counts and
  open-ticket counts for a whole page in four statements; `user_out(db, u, facts)` reads from it.
  A test asserts the batched output equals the per-user one for every user. `/admin/keys`,
  `/payments`, `/tickets`, `/waitlist` take `?limit=&offset=` (default 200, max 1000); the portal
  client passes nothing and gets the first page, so nothing there changes today.
- **`create-admin` refuses an existing account** without `--reset`, and says to use `promote`.
- **The desktop's offline grace expires in a running app.** `licence.ts`'s `check()` returned
  `offline` from a re-check even past the week, so only a relaunch ever locked; it now returns
  `invalid`/`unknown` the way its docstring said, and `firstCheck` is gone because nothing
  distinguished on it any more.
- **CORS regex** — a comment in `main.py` saying the regex decides who may *read* a response and
  the `SameSite=Lax` cookie plus the bearer token are the controls. The regex is unchanged.
