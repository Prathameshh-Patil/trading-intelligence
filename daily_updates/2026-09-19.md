## 10. ⛔ What this does NOT unblock, and the number that decides it

**No bars were pulled and no Track C step ran.** Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters.

**(1) Arithmetic. HTTP is a bounded-window transport, not an archive transport.** ~3–5 minutes a
day against S3's one object: a month is an hour, and **the walk-forward's five years is ~1,776
trading days ≈ 120 hours**. S3 remains the bulk path at ~$0.12 and **the credential task is not
retired by any of this** — it is now worth ten minutes instead of blocking a layer.

**(2) The feed degraded under exactly the use this was built for, and the attempt is recorded
rather than the intention.** A real `spot.pull` of 2026-08 over HTTP **aborted on the first day**:
2026-08-01 is a Saturday and hour 0 returned 503 through all five attempts. **503 is overloaded —
it is returned both by the throttle and, apparently, by hours the market was shut** — and the two
cannot be told apart from the status code. The probe written to separate them (rest 90s, then a
known-good hour, a Saturday hour, and a second known-good hour) **never completed its first
request**: after ~120 requests the feed stopped answering rather than refusing. That is E0's
finding in a new costume, and it is why nothing here claims the HTTP path is a solved transport.

**`fetch_day` does not and will not treat a 503 as an empty hour.** It would make weekend days pull
cleanly and it would also, on a throttled weekday, write a day with a hole that reads as a calm
session forever after. **Guessing which 503 is which is the one thing this module must not do.**
The fix is §11's: state the week as a fact about the instrument instead.

## 11. The trading week, stated rather than inferred — `SHUT` and `BOUNDARY`

**Varad's call, 19 Sep: take the HTTP bounded window, and solve the weekend at the pull level.**
So `dukascopy.py` now carries which UTC hours the feed has no object for, by weekday, and
`fetch_day` reads the week instead of reading a status code.

**The DST problem, and why the table is two tables.** `instruments.XAUUSD` records the week as
"~22:00 Sun to 21:00 Fri UTC", and the tilde is the whole difficulty: under EDT the week runs
22:00 Sun → 21:00 Fri with a 21:00–22:00 daily break, and under EST every boundary moves an hour
later. A DST-naive table would skip hours that hold data on half the calendar.

- **`SHUT` is the *intersection* of the two regimes** — Saturday entire, Sunday 00:00–21:59, Friday
  22:00–23:59. These hours have no object under either, so they are **never requested at all**.
  **A weekend day now costs zero requests**, which is both the fix and a ~28% cut in the requests
  a month needs.
- **`BOUNDARY` is where the regimes disagree** — the daily break (21:00 or 22:00) and the two
  weekly boundaries themselves. There and *only* there, a persistent 503 is accepted as "no
  object". **Everywhere else it still aborts the day unwritten.** At most two hours a day.

**The invariant is intact where it matters.** Every hour that must hold data under both regimes is
still fetched, and still aborts the day if it does not arrive. What was relaxed is exactly the set
of hours where absence is a legitimate answer — and that set is enumerated in the source, not
inferred at runtime.

**Three tests, and one of them is the pair's shape:** a Saturday makes no request at all (the
`AssertionError` fires if it does); a dead 21:00 on a Thursday yields a 23-hour day while a dead
14:00 on the same Thursday raises; and no hour appears in both tables, since an hour in both would
be skipped without ever being asked for. **668 passing, +8 on the session.**

⚠️ **Owed, and not yet done: verifying the week against the archive it just pulled.** The table is
written from the venue's published hours and the repo's own prose, not measured. The first full
month on disk can check it — the first and last tick of each week, and where the daily gap
actually falls — and until that runs, `SHUT` is an assumption with tests around its shape rather
than a measurement.

## 14. The 7-digit CVD — what actually breaks, and what was believed to

**The number never overflowed.** The panel was driven as a plain page in Chrome at 380px (the
window width `tauri.conf.json` fixes), licensed with a real `vh_live_` key minted through the
portal, and the mock's *7-digit CVD* button pressed on both Order Flow and Analyze.
`+1,234,567` measures **174px in a 308px box**; measured across the whole font stack in case
Inter is missing on a machine — `system-ui` 174, Arial 151, Inter/SF 137 — nothing exceeds
half the card. `document.querySelector('.view').scrollWidth === clientWidth` on both views;
zero elements with a right edge past 380.

**What does break is vertical, and it was already broken.** `.view` is `position: absolute;
inset: 0; display: flex; flex-direction: column; overflow-y: auto` — a fixed-height column that
scrolls. A flex item's `min-height` is `auto` *unless it clips its own overflow*, in which case
it is 0 and flex may shrink the item to make the column fit its height. `.feed-meta` has
`overflow: hidden`, so **the `GC · 1m · 0 gaps · 18:16 ET` row on Order Flow has been rendering
at 0px since W1D2** — it is in the DOM, `innerText` returns it, and it is invisible. The first
attempt at hardening the CVD card (`overflow: hidden` on `.cvd-card`) collapsed the card to its
label the same way, which is the screenshot below and the moment the mechanism became clear.

![The CVD card squashed to its label; the meta row above the credentials card missing](assets/2026-09-19-cvd-card-squashed.jpg)

Fix, `theme.css`: `.view > * { flex-shrink: 0; }` — the column scrolls, nothing shrinks —
and `.cvd-sub` gets the same `nowrap`/ellipsis as `.cvd-value` because Analyze appends
`· N contracts` to it. The card no longer carries `overflow: hidden`.

**And the mock now holds the bar.** `emitWideCvd` calls `clearTimers()` before emitting, so the
7-digit bar stays until *Resume*. Previously the next replayed bar overwrote it in under a
second — which is why 3 Sep's row was closed on account with no screenshot after four capture
attempts, and why "it looked fine for a moment" was the only evidence. Emitting after the
clear still arms the stale watchdog, so the pill turns `Stale 47s` honestly rather than
claiming a live feed that is not ticking.

![After: meta row visible, +1,234,567 held, pill stale](assets/2026-09-19-cvd-7digit-held.jpg)

## 15. S7 — "frozen" was a two-hour wall clock under a 10× replay

`createMockForecaster` set `connectedAt = now()` the first time `getForecaster()` ran — which
is the first time the Forecast view mounted — and `warming()` compared **wall-clock** time to
`WARM_UP_MINUTES = 120`. Three things followed. The mock replays 2026-07-16 at 10×, so two hours
of bars arrive in twelve real minutes, and the forecaster ignored all of them. The view kept its
own `connectedAt` per mount, so the progress bar reset to zero on every navigation while the
forecaster's clock did not. Every relaunch restarted the two hours even when the feed had been
up all session. Under `reach.cell_of`'s own rule — *`rv_slope` needs two hours of bars* — the
warm-up is a property of bars received, and the code measured seconds.

**Fix:** `Forecaster.warmUp(): { elapsedMs, requiredMs }` is on the seam (`forecast.ts`); the
mock subscribes to `engine.onBar` and counts `lastBarT - firstBarT`; `feed.ts` constructs the
forecaster when the feed connects, not when a view opens; `ForecastView` renders the
forecaster's number and keeps only a 1s clock to re-read it. The ETA copy is
*first forecast after 118m more of feed · ~12m at 10× replay*, the second clause only while
the mock controls exist. `finishWarmUp` and `restartWarmUp` still work; `Mode` loses
`"warming"`, which nothing ever set.

**One more case, found by the reload:** the replay restarting made `bar.t` go backwards and the
countdown read *127m more of feed* over a full bar (`elapsed` negative; the negative width was

same. Feed time going backwards is now treated as a reconnect and the warm-up restarts from

## 17. E4 — built to §17, tested on fixtures, not run on the archive

**`hmm.py`.** A k-state full-covariance Gaussian HMM in numpy: log-space forward/backward,
Baum-Welch, Viterbi, `filtered` (P(S_t | x_1..t)) and `posteriors` (smoothed). Not `hmmlearn`:
the lockfile is frozen and a dependency is its own PR, and the test that proves this is
stronger than a changelog — **on a four-bar chain the likelihood equals the sum over all
sixteen state paths to 1e-9**. EM's trace is pinned non-decreasing; a planted two-state chain
is recovered with means within 0.15 and 97% Viterbi agreement; **`filtered[:60]` is unchanged
when rows 60+ are altered and `posteriors[:60]` is not** — the guard that keeps a smoothed
posterior out of a feature. Segments restart from `pi`: a session gap is not a transition.
