## `services/signal-data/strategies.py` — 191 lines, 11 tests

§7 of the [selector design](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-25-gc-strategy-selector-design.md)
calls this file `strategies/gc.py`. **It was built flat, as `strategies.py`, and that is a
deliberate departure from the spec.** A package directory for one module buys nothing while GC is
the only instrument — `NQ` was dropped, and the rest of `services/signal-data/` is flat and imports
flat (`backtest.py` does `from s1 import TICK`). Rule of three: no package until a second
instrument exists. Agreed before writing.

Two layers.

**State features** — `delta_z`, `cvd_slope`, `absorption`, `bar_imbalance`. §2's `regimes.py` needs
the same four, so they are built once and read twice rather than re-derived there.

**Four candidates** — `delta_outlier`, `cvd_divergence`, `absorption_fade`, `footprint_stack`. Each
is `(bars, *, thresholds) -> Series of +1/-1/0` aligned to `bars.index`, which is exactly what
`backtest.evaluate` already consumes. `backtest.py` and `s1.py` were **not touched.**

### Entry-only, and the exit is the horizon

The design says "entry condition, exit condition". `backtest.evaluate` has no exit mechanism — it
measures the clock horizons and reports MFE/MAE. Rather than grow a tested file, Stage 1 stays
entry-only: **MFE/MAE already say what a stop or a target would have done without committing to
one**, and a stop plus a target per strategy would be two more numbers Part B has to commit and a
multiplied search space that §6 exists to guard. Stage 1 is a kill gate; it only has to answer
whether there is directional edge at all. If a candidate survives its committed bar, the exit
engine gets built then.

### The prose is a proposal, not a transcription

Each strategy's docstring states its rule in words. **That wording is a literal reading of the
family, not how anyone actually trades it.** Part B is where it gets confirmed or rewritten. The
one most likely to be wrong is flagged in the file itself: `absorption_fade` assumes heavy delta
into a tight bar means the aggressor was trapped and the resting side won. The same bar reads as
*continuation* if you think the aggressor is early. If it is continuation, the fix is one sign
change — but it is a decision, not a detail.

**No threshold in the file has a default.** Calling a strategy without its numbers raises

## The three ways a feature of this kind lies, one test each

All three produce signal series that look entirely plausible, which is why they are tests rather
than comments.

| Trap | Test |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Session-scoped statistics are lookahead.** A 09:30 bar z-scored against the session's own standard deviation has read the afternoon | Two sessions, the second opening at delta 1000. Session two's opening bars come back `NaN` — the window stopped at the break instead of scoring 1000 against session one |
| **Rows are not minutes.** `minute_bars` drops empty minutes, so `.rolling(30)` is thirty *bars*. Same trap `backtest.py` already solved for horizons | Bars at minutes 0,1,2 then a 100-minute gap. At minute 100 the trailing 30 minutes hold one bar, so it is `NaN` — a row window would have handed it three |
| **Reading a bar later than the one you fill on**, the only one here that cannot be found by looking at the output | Mutate bars 50-59 to absurd values, recompute, assert bars 0-49 are unchanged for every feature |

Two smaller ones got tests because both turn the *strongest* case into a dropped row: a bar where
`high == low` is the purest absorption there is and must not divide by zero; a lone print with
nothing resting opposite is **not** an infinite footprint imbalance.

`bar_imbalance` converts price to integer tick levels before grouping, because `99.9 / 0.10` is not
`999` in binary floating point and a float key silently splits a price level in two.

## The absorption ratio distribution, and the defect it exposed

Varad asked for it, knowing it is the contamination §6 warns about. It was printed with that said
once and not relitigated. **It turned out to expose a bug rather than a threshold**, which is the
argument for having looked.

`absorption` = `|delta| / range_in_ticks`, over the 1,379 bars of 2026-07-16:

| p50 | p75 | p90 | p95 | p99 | p99.5 | p99.9 | max |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.61 | 1.11 | 1.80 | 2.44 | 4.35 | 5.00 | 7.86 | **10.67** |

The placeholder was **500**, so it was off by roughly fifty times. For scale: median bar range is
**15 ticks** and median `|delta|` is **8 contracts**, so on GC 1-minute bars the denominator
dominates and the ratio lives between 0 and 11.

### The defect

The eight highest-ratio bars in the session:

```
timestamp                  delta   range_ticks   ratio   volume
2026-07-15 22:29:00+00:00     32           3.0   10.67       32
2026-07-15 22:16:00+00:00    -16           2.0    8.00       16
2026-07-16 09:16:00+00:00    107          14.0    7.64      180
2026-07-15 23:27:00+00:00     -6           1.0    6.00        6
2026-07-15 22:40:00+00:00     29           5.0    5.80       37
2026-07-16 10:03:00+00:00    -66          13.0    5.08      128
2026-07-16 12:48:00+00:00    105          21.0    5.00      294
2026-07-15 22:13:00+00:00     -5           1.0    5.00        5
```

**Half of them are 5-, 6-, 16- and 32-lot bars between 22:13 and 23:27 UTC** — the Globex-open
dead zone. A 5-contract bar in a one-tick range scores 5.00, exactly what a 294-contract bar in a
21-tick range scores.

The ratio is **scale-free**, so on its own it selects for **illiquidity** — precisely the opposite
of absorption, which means *large* size that failed to move price. Left alone, this rule would
have gone into Stage 1 firing mostly on an empty overnight market, produced a plausible-looking
distribution, and told nobody.

### The fix

`absorption_fade` now requires **both** `min_ratio` and `min_delta`. The size floor is not a
refinement; it is what makes the rule mean what its name says. Both are still Varad's numbers,
still with no defaults. A test asserts a 5-lot bar in a one-tick range does **not** fire while a
900-lot bar in the same range does. **28 tests, `ruff` and `mypy` still clean.**

### The question this leaves for Part B

**Absorption is classically a price-level phenomenon, not a time-bar one** — size stacking at one
price and failing to break it. `compute_delta_cvd.py`'s `footprint()` already aggregates delta by
price level and is the natural home for that reading. `|delta| / bar range` is a proxy for it, and
with a median bar range of 15 ticks it is a loose one.

So Part B has a prior question before it has a number: **is the time-bar reading the right rule at
all, or should absorption be measured at the price level like the footprint already does?** That
is a rule decision, not a threshold, and it is the trader's the same way the rest is.

Only the feature's own distribution. **No forward return, MFE, MAE or hit rate was computed for
any candidate**, so nothing about whether absorption *pays* has been seen, and §6.1's gate on

## `stage1.py` — the runner, 118 lines, 6 tests

The file that turns Part B into results on the day Part B is written. It holds **no thresholds of
its own**: the caller supplies each candidate already bound to its bars, plus the numbers it was
committed with. Binding `bars` (and `ticks`, for the one candidate that needs them) with
`functools.partial` at the call site is what lets two strategies of different arities go through
one loop.

One tidy row per strategy × variant × horizon, N on every one:

| Variant | What it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| `actual` | the rule as committed |
| `null` | §6.3's stage-1 null — random entry, matched count, holding period **and side mix** |
| `thr-20%` / `thr+20%` | A5 |
| `filtered` | A6, **and only when a filter is supplied.** Its absence is the honest report that none has been |

`decision_filter` is a parameter, not a stub file — a no-op module that only forwards would be a
layer pretending the seam is wired when it is not. A test passes a filter that vetoes everything
and asserts the resulting `n` is 0, which is A6's point: a filter that improves the numbers by
deleting the sample has to be visible doing it.

**No walk-forward split here, deliberately.** A1's split is about regimes, regimes are Stage 2, and
Stage 2 exists only if Stage 1's kill gate is cleared. Nothing in this file fits anything.

`tests/conftest.py` is new: `bars_at` was about to become its third copy, which is where a
duplicated helper stops being worth tolerating. `test_backtest.py` and `test_strategies.py` now
import it. **34 tests pass, `ruff` and `mypy` clean over 9 files.**

### A5 does not mean what it looks like it means for two of the four

A5 exists because session delta is method-dependent at ~15–20%, and the design's example of the
cost is *"a rule that fires at delta > 200 fires somewhere between roughly 170 and 240."* That
reasoning holds for a threshold denominated in **contracts**. It does not hold for a z-score.

A z-score divides by the standard deviation of the same series, so scaling every delta by 1.2
cancels top and bottom exactly. `test_a_z_threshold_does_not_inherit_the_delta_error_a_magnitude_one_does`
asserts it: `delta_outlier`'s signals are **identical** on a frame whose delta is read 20% larger,
while `absorption_fade`'s change. `footprint_stack`'s `ratio` is invariant for the same reason —
both sides scale.

So: **`cvd_divergence` (`min_slope`) and `absorption_fade` (`min_ratio`, `min_delta`) carry the
inherited error. `delta_outlier` and `footprint_stack` are structurally immune to it.**

This matters for how A5's output gets read. On the fixture, moving `delta_outlier`'s `z` by ±20%
swings its sample count from 19 to 55 and 12 — which *looks* like the error bar biting hard, and
is not. It is threshold sensitivity, which is worth knowing, but reporting it as the inherited
±15–20% would **overstate** the uncertainty on that strategy. `PERTURB` in `stage1.py` says which
keys move and why each is in or out.

Run end to end on the fixture with throwaway thresholds. Same precedent as 25 Aug: exercising the

stage1.run    0.35s      report shape (48, 15) = 4 strategies x 4 variants x 3 horizons

The null reproduced `actual`'s N exactly on all four candidates, which is `random_entries` matching
count and side mix doing its job. **24 of 48 cells clear A4's floor of 30** on a single session.
